# Team 2 Findings: Found-Document Worldbuilding Narrative
## Date: 2026-03-16
## Researcher: Team Member 2

---

## Framing Note

This angle covers how projects like Mystery Flesh Pit National Park, SCP Foundation, House of Leaves, The Holders, Welcome to Night Vale, and analog horror (Local 58, The Mandela Catalogue) create narrative from documents alone — no traditional narrator making discoveries, just accumulated materials that together build toward revelation.

The core challenge our series faces: we already use found documents (diary entries, engineering reports, testimony, classified memos). The problem is that we treat them as evidence supporting a thesis, not as the structural skeleton of a story. These reference works solved that problem. Here is what they found.

---

## Battle-Tested Approaches

### 1. The Epistolary Stack: Documents That Together Know More Than Any Single One

- **What:** Multiple document formats (diary, letter, newspaper clipping, official report, testimony) are presented in sequence. No document alone reveals the full picture. The reader constructs the truth by comparing and triangulating across sources.
- **Evidence:** Bram Stoker's Dracula (1897) is the foundational proof-of-concept, still in active use: Dracula Daily (Substack newsletter, 500,000+ subscribers in 2022) serialized the novel in real time and created mass community engagement — demonstrating that epistolary structure alone can carry a 90-day arc with no additional narrative scaffolding. The novel layers Jonathan Harker's journal, Mina's diary, Dr. Seward's phonograph recordings, newspaper clippings, and Van Helsing's memoranda.
- **Source:** [Dracula Daily and the Tradition of Serialized Literature — Confluence, Gallatin NYU](https://confluence.gallatin.nyu.edu/context/independent-project/dracula-daily-the-tradition-of-serialized-literature); [Stoker's Epistolary Approach — Gilliam Writers Group](https://www.gilliamwritersgroup.com/blog/stokers-epistolary-approach-crafting-a-multifaceted-narrative); [Dracula: A Love Letter to Letters — Carterhaugh School](https://carterhaughschool.com/dracula-a-love-letter-to-letters/)
- **Fits our case because:** Our series already uses multiple fabricated sources. The missing piece is treating them as a *stack* rather than as illustrations. Each document in a stack should carry knowledge the others lack, so the listener must hold all of them together to understand what happened. The revelation is the synthesis, not any single document.
- **Tradeoffs:** Requires careful information architecture before writing — you have to know exactly what each document knows and doesn't know. Harder to write than an essay because you cannot just present the conclusion. Also: single-narrator audio flattens voice differentiation between documents, which is one of epistolary fiction's key tools. Requires tonal and structural variation instead of voice variation.

### 2. The SCP Addenda Stack: Clinical Description + Incident Log + Interview = Escalating Revelation

- **What:** A document begins with dry containment procedures (clinical, distancing), then moves into incident logs and interview transcripts where the actual horror unfolds in real time through accumulated events. Each addendum reveals one more layer of what went wrong and why it matters.
- **Evidence:** SCP Foundation has used this format since 2007-2008, producing tens of thousands of articles at community scale. The format's effectiveness is so well-established that its narrative mechanics have been formally analyzed by contributors: the progression is explicitly described as "how to handle a thing → what the thing is → important information → less important information → tangentially relevant information," with narrative embedded in the incident/interview layers. SCP entries routinely achieve 10,000+ words through this stacking method while maintaining tension throughout.
- **Source:** [Adding Narrative Structure to SCP Articles — SCP Foundation Forum](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/forum/t-10557450/adding-narrative-structure-to-scp-articles); [Seeing SCP as a Narrative Protocol — Scenes With Simon](https://sceneswithsimon.com/p/seeing-scp-as-a-narrative-protocol); [The SCP Essay on Narrative — scp-wiki.wikidot.com](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/you-already-know-what-the-fuck-is-going-on)
- **Fits our case because:** This is the most directly applicable structure for our format. An episode could open with a neutral "engineering assessment" or "archival memo" establishing the subject clinically, then move into diary fragments, then incident reports, then redacted testimony — each layer cracking the clinical frame wider. The Tesla episodes have the raw material for this. The clinical opening establishes credibility. The incident logs are where the story lives.
- **Tradeoffs:** The SCP community analysis notes that "note at the end" and "updated file version" reveals are the most overused devices — they work but feel cheap. Also: the format front-loads dry material, which risks losing listeners in the first 10 minutes before the incident logs begin. Must establish enough unease in the clinical section to justify the wait.

### 3. Mystery Flesh Pit's Archaeological Layering: Documents That Imply a Timeline

- **What:** Documents are presented as artifacts from different eras (early exploration, peak operations, post-catastrophe), and the accumulation of these artifacts implies a complete history without narrating it directly. The reader constructs the timeline by inference.
- **Evidence:** Mystery Flesh Pit National Park (Trevor Roberts, ongoing since 2018 on Tumblr) creates an entire history of a national park built inside a living superorganism through brochures, trail signs, spa menus, safety manuals, government memos, and disaster reports — no continuous narrative prose, no narrator explaining what happened. Roberts' 2007 Disaster Report uses minute-by-minute bureaucratic timestamps (9:51 PM, 9:51:45 PM, 9:52 PM) to create escalating panic entirely through official record format. The project has generated a book deal, RPG supplement, and dedicated fandom — proof the document-only structure scales.
- **Source:** [Welcome to Mystery Flesh Pit National Park — Reactor Magazine](https://reactormag.com/welcome-to-mystery-flesh-pit-national-park-one-redditors-colossal-feat-of-worldbuilding/); [World Building Artifacts: Mystery Flesh Pit — Tristan Markwell Substack](https://tristanmarkwell.substack.com/p/world-building-artifacts-mystery); [Mystery Flesh Pit 2007 Disaster Report](https://www.mysteryfleshpitnationalpark.com/post/643404189591109632/2007-disaster-report-text-version); [Mystery Flesh Pit Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mystery_Flesh_Pit_National_Park)
- **Fits our case because:** This is the structural solution to the "collection vs. story" problem. When documents come from different time periods and different institutional contexts, the listener automatically asks: *what changed? What happened between these two documents?* The gap between documents becomes the story engine. A 1899 Tesla laboratory notebook entry plus a 1943 Army Signal Corps memo plus a 1987 CERN internal review plus a 2011 blog post from a physics student who disappeared — the listener builds the narrative from the white space between them.
- **Tradeoffs:** Roberts' format works in visual media (his documents are images with National Park Service design language). In audio, the format-shift signal has to come from tonal and structural variation rather than visual differentiation. The listener cannot see that one document is a brochure and another is a classified memo — they have to *hear* it. Requires highly distinct register shifts and explicit framing.

### 4. Night Vale's Cumulative Strangeness: Bureaucratic Normality as Dread Delivery System

- **What:** Every event is reported in the tone appropriate to routine civic announcements. Horror is delivered at the same pitch as traffic updates. Accumulation of normalized strange things — rather than escalation toward one climax — is what creates the atmosphere that makes individual revelations land harder when they arrive.
- **Evidence:** Welcome to Night Vale (Night Vale Presents, 2012–present) has produced 200+ episodes using this structure, won multiple Podcast Awards, and expanded into novels and live tours. Academic analysis describes each episode as "cumulative rather than delivering an obviously sequential narrative — a cloud of impressions." Episodes are explicitly designed for entry at any point; no episode requires prior knowledge. The "bureaucratic care" tone — Garrison Keillor as cosmic horror — has been studied in academic contexts and identified as the central mechanism.
- **Source:** [At Home with the Weird: Dark Eco-Discourse in Tanis and Welcome to Night Vale — Revenant Journal](https://www.revenantjournal.com/contents/at-home-with-the-weird-dark-eco-discourse-in-tanis-and-welcome-to-night-vale-danielle-barrios-oneill-falmouth-university-and-michael-collins-university-of-kent/); [Welcome to Night Vale Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welcome_to_Night_Vale); [Welcome to Night Vale — Find That Pod](https://findthatpod.com/welcome-to-night-vale-podcast-review/)
- **Fits our case because:** This is the tonal model for how to deliver fabricated sources. Our documents should read in institutional register — not elevated horror prose, but the voice appropriate to what the document *is*. An engineering report sounds like an engineering report. A congressional testimony sounds like congressional testimony. The horror comes from what such documents are calmly describing, not from how dramatically the prose describes it. This is how you make a 60-90 minute TTS episode not feel like an essay — the bureaucratic register itself creates cognitive dissonance, and cognitive dissonance creates unease.
- **Tradeoffs:** Night Vale works because Cecil Palmer is a character with personality — the bureaucratic tone is filtered through a specific human presence. Our single TTS narrator has no personality of its own. The tonal anchor has to be the *documents themselves*, not the narrator's character. This means the document selection and sequencing have to do work that a narrator's persona does in Night Vale.

### 5. Dracula's Dramatic Irony Engine: The Reader Assembles What Characters Cannot

- **What:** Different documents are narrated by people with limited individual knowledge. The reader/listener, having access to all the documents, understands the danger before any single character does. This gap — the reader knowing more than the characters — is the core engine of sustained dread.
- **Evidence:** In Dracula, Stoker uses the mosaic of documents specifically so that Van Helsing doesn't understand what Jonathan understood until chapter 17, and Jonathan doesn't understand what Mina's diary revealed until it's almost too late. Readers who understand vampire lore "know more than the characters" throughout — dramatic irony running for 400 pages. Literary critics identify this as the primary tension mechanism: "No single narrator sees the whole picture" (Carterhaugh School analysis, accessed 2026).
- **Source:** [Stoker's Epistolary Approach — Gilliam Writers Group](https://www.gilliamwritersgroup.com/blog/stokers-epistolary-approach-crafting-a-multifaceted-narrative); [The Epistolary Nature of Bram Stoker's Dracula — The Curious Reader](https://www.thecuriousreader.in/features/dracula-epistolary-novel/); [Three Essential Writing Techniques from Stoker's Dracula — Vocal Media](https://vocal.media/horror/three-essential-writing-techniques-from-stoker-s-dracula-and-the-epistolary-narrative)
- **Fits our case because:** Our fabricated sources are narrated by people who do not understand what they witnessed. The 1901 diary entry writer doesn't know she's describing a Mandela Effect. The 1943 Signal Corps officer doesn't know the interference pattern he's filing a report about will match the geometry Tesla described in 1899. The listener, having heard both, understands the connection. This is not the narrator telling the listener what it means — it is the listener discovering it themselves. That is the difference between essay and story.
- **Tradeoffs:** Requires careful construction: the person writing each document must be believably limited in their understanding — they can't accidentally state the thesis. They see what they see and report it in their own institutional language. The connection must be implicit, not stated. This is a harder craft standard than our current approach, where the narrator synthesizes and explains.

## Novel Approaches

### 6. The Mandela Catalogue's Institutional Compromise: When the Document Itself Is Evidence of the Threat

- **What:** Documents don't just describe the threat — they show that the institutions producing official documents have already been compromised by it. Emergency broadcast guidelines instruct operators not to help callers reporting the anomaly "no matter how frantic their screams." The document's content reveals its own corruption.
- **Why it's interesting:** This converts the document from a *report about* a threat to *evidence of* a threat. When an official source tells you not to look at something, the prohibition is the horror. This is what makes MFPNP's disaster report so effective: the recommendations at the end — authorizing continued extraction after 700 deaths — reveal that the institution has normalized the catastrophe. The document's conclusion proves the system is broken.
- **Evidence:** The Mandela Catalogue (Alex Kister, 2021–present) uses this technique throughout: "official instructional video for 911 emergency hotline operators" instructs staff with directives like "Do not help a caller reporting an alternate encounter, no matter how frantic their screams are." The institutional voice is the threat vector. Kister identifies tension-building as his "biggest strength" and the series has generated significant fandom analysis (Nestor's Notes, Oreate AI Blog).
- **Source:** [Ghosts in the Machine: The Mandela Catalogue — Nestor's Notes](https://nestorsnotes.com/articles/ghosts-in-the-machine-alex-kister-the-mandela-catalogue); [Beyond the Static: Unpacking The Mandela Catalogue — Oreate AI Blog](https://www.oreateai.com/blog/beyond-the-static-unpacking-the-eerie-world-of-the-mandela-catalogues-alternates/0f47b11df1a6c342bacb3b68e7e2af24); [Analog Horror Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_horror)
- **Fits our case because:** A 1917 Westinghouse corporate memo that *declines* to investigate an anomalous resonance pattern in Tesla's Colorado Springs equipment — written in the language of liability management — is more terrifying than a memo that describes what happened. The document's evasion is the evidence. The thing being suppressed is visible in the shape of the suppression.
- **Risks:** Requires the writer to know exactly what the institution is hiding and *why* it would hide it in this particular way. If the evasion is unmotivated, it reads as authorial convenience rather than institutional logic. Also: a single episode can only do this once before it becomes a formula.

### 7. The SCP Detective Method: Strategic Gaps That Force Listener Inference

- **What:** Documents contain structured absences — information that *should* be in this kind of document but isn't — that force the listener to supply an explanation. The explanation they supply is always worse than anything the document could state.
- **Why it's interesting:** This weaponizes the listener's imagination rather than the writer's description. As Andrew Stanton (Pixar) phrased it: "Storytelling is the well-organized absence of information." SCP's own analysis identifies this as the "Detective Method" — readers "speculate about events the Foundation didn't witness." The horror is what the Foundation cannot explain, not what it can.
- **Evidence:** MFPNP's disaster report uses this with "The [CONTINGENCY MEASURE]" — the document refers to a response protocol that is entirely redacted. The reader does not know what it was. They know only that it was used and was insufficient. Mystery Flesh Pit's Fanlore entry notes that the project leaves key elements "shrouded in mystery, partially because science hasn't been able to explain certain aspects of the Pit." Lovecraftian horror scholarship confirms: "true terror is about atmosphere, mystery, and slow realization" rather than explicit reveals (Static Dread: The Lighthouse review, Neon Lights Media, 2024).
- **Source:** [Adding Narrative Structure to SCP Articles — SCP Foundation Forum](https://scp-wiki.wikidot.com/forum/t-10557450/adding-narrative-structure-to-scp-articles); [Mystery Flesh Pit Fanlore](https://fanlore.org/wiki/Mystery_Flesh_Pit_National_Park); [Static Dread: The Lighthouse — Neon Lights Media](https://www.neonlightsmedia.com/blog/static-dread-the-lighthouse-review-lovecraft-papers-please-full)
- **Fits our case because:** Tesla's actual historical record has genuine gaps — periods of silence, experiments that left no record, correspondence that was seized, patents that were filed and withdrawn. A fabricated document can cite "the Colorado Springs notebooks, pages 112-118 (missing since 1899)" and the absence of those pages is the story. The listener knows something was on those pages. The document knowing about the gap without filling it is more effective than any description of what those pages contained.
- **Risks:** Requires discipline — the gap must be *specific*, not vague. "Something happened" is not a gap; it's a placeholder. "The temperature readings for the night of July 4th are absent, but the instrument log shows the thermometer was recording until 11:47 PM" is a gap. The specificity of the surrounding data makes the absence legible.

### 8. The Holders Format: Second-Person Instructions as Narrative Propulsion

- **What:** Documents are written as instructions addressed directly to the reader/listener in second person: "You must go to [location] and say exactly these words." The fictional frame positions the reader as an active participant rather than an observer. Each instruction implies what will happen if the instructions are not followed exactly — and what has happened to those who preceded you.
- **Why it's interesting:** This converts documentary distance into narrative immediacy without adding a character. The second person "you" does the work that a protagonist does in traditional narrative.
- **Evidence:** The Holders series (2006–ongoing, community collaborative) uses second-person instruction format throughout: "Go to any city, any town, any village. Find the nearest mental institution or hospital. Approach the front desk and say, 'I would like to speak to the Holder of [X].'" The instructions themselves carry all backstory and consequence through implication. TV Tropes notes: "The majority of the stories are written as instructions, as if given to the reader by a Mysterious Benefactor. Fan-written Holders dive straight into the realm of horror-fantasy adventure."
- **Source:** [The Holders Series — TV Tropes](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Literature/TheHoldersSeries); [The Holders — Bogleech](https://bogleech.com/halloween/hall17-theholders)
- **Fits our case because:** A Tesla episode could use a single second-person document as a structural anchor — a set of instructions that someone apparently followed, with annotations or marginalia showing what actually happened when they did. Or: an operator's manual for a piece of Tesla's equipment, with handwritten corrections from multiple users over decades, each correction implying what the previous instruction failed to account for.
- **Risks:** Second person is unusual in documentary audio — it breaks the "found document" illusion slightly because actual found documents are rarely addressed to "you." Must be motivated (a training manual, an instruction sheet left for a successor, a letter that begins "if you are reading this"). Also: TTS delivery of second-person is potentially disorienting for listeners not expecting direct address.

## Emerging Approaches

### 9. Analog Horror's Format Escalation: Each Document Is in a More Personal Register Than the Last

- **What:** The sequence of documents moves from most institutional (official broadcast, organizational memo) to most personal (diary, recording made in extremis), creating emotional acceleration even without plot acceleration. The format shift is the escalation.
- **Momentum:** Analog horror as a genre is growing rapidly — The Mandela Catalogue, Local 58, The Walten Files all use this structure. The genre emerged from YouTube creepypasta around 2015 (Local 58, Kris Straub) and has expanded significantly since 2021. Multiple academic analyses appeared in 2023-2024 studying the genre's narrative techniques.
- **Source:** [LOCAL58 and the Birth of Analog Horror — Dread Central](https://www.dreadcentral.com/editorials/534205/local58-and-the-birth-of-analog-horror/); [Analog Horror Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_horror); [Exploring the Distortion of Humanity in Analog Horror — UNCA journal, 2023](https://unca-urcap.org/static/journal_spring_2023/Serdula_2023Journal_FINAL.pdf)
- **Fits our case because:** An episode that opens with a government spectrum-allocation memo (maximally institutional), progresses through a corporate engineering report, then a professional diary entry, then a private letter, then a handwritten note with no salutation — each document is in a more vulnerable register than the last. The listener feels the person getting closer to something they cannot maintain professional distance from. The format is the emotional arc.
- **Maturity risk:** Analog horror's techniques are optimized for visual media (VHS aesthetics, video glitches) — the format shifts are visible in a way audio cannot replicate. The audio version must use prose register shifts, document-framing language ("the following is a transcript of"), and tone variation to create equivalent differentiation. This has not been systematically tested at scale in audio-only long-form yet.

### 10. The Sonic Serial Suspense Framework (S3F): Epistemic Gaps + Affective Stakes + Temporal Delay

- **What:** A recently proposed academic framework identifying that suspense in podcast fiction requires three simultaneous elements: an information gap (something unknown), emotional stakes (reason to care about what's unknown), and temporal delay (postponement of resolution). All three must be present; any one alone is insufficient.
- **Momentum:** The framework appears in a 2025 academic paper from Latent Scholar analyzing podcast fiction narrative mechanics. It synthesizes existing suspense theory into audio-specific application.
- **Source:** [Seriality and Suspense in Podcast Fiction: Toward a Conceptual Framework — Latent Scholar](https://latentscholar.org/seriality-and-suspense-in-podcast-fiction-toward-a-conceptual-framework-for-new-narrative-forms-in-audio-media/)
- **Fits our case because:** This framework is diagnostic. Our current episodes have information gaps (what happened?) but low affective stakes (we don't know whose story this is — there is no person at risk) and compressed temporal delay (we present information and then immediately resolve what it means). The framework predicts our episodes feel like essays: we have gaps but no stakes and no delay. Fix: each document must introduce a person whose situation is unresolved, and the resolution must be withheld until the episode's final document. The gap, the stakes, and the delay all depend on establishing a human subject who is navigating something dangerous.
- **Maturity risk:** The paper is recent (2025) and the framework has not yet been widely tested against long-form single-episode audio at the 60-90 minute range. It was developed primarily in the context of serialized fiction with inter-episode gaps, which creates natural temporal delay. Within a single episode, temporal delay must be manufactured through structural means.

## Gaps and Unknowns

**Gap 1: Audio-specific translation of visual format cues.** All major found-document projects (MFPNP, analog horror, SCP's image-based reveals) rely heavily on visual differentiation between document types. No substantial body of evidence exists for how to create equivalent document-format signals in audio-only, single-narrator TTS delivery. This is the central unsolved problem and the place where this research reaches its limit. It will require empirical testing by the production team.

**Gap 2: Single-episode vs. serial accumulation.** Most found-document worldbuilding operates at serial scale — MFPNP adds artifacts over years, SCP canons link entries across months, analog horror volumes build across multiple releases. Very few examples achieve found-document narrative arc within a single work at 60-90 minutes. House of Leaves does it at novel length. Dracula does it at novel length. The question of whether these techniques compress to a single 90-minute audio episode without requiring prior context is partially answered (they compress, but the compression requires more explicit framing of document provenance) but not definitively proven.

**Gap 3: The affective stakes problem.** All the reference works that work as stories — as opposed to interesting collections — have a person we care about whose situation is unresolved. In analog horror it is usually someone we see in found footage. In Dracula it is Mina and Jonathan. In House of Leaves it is multiple people whose fates remain open. In MFPNP the 700 dead from the 2007 catastrophe create a named tragedy. Our series has no character at risk. The documents describe events, but no person is trying to survive them. This is a structural problem that found-document techniques alone cannot fully solve. They can create dread; they cannot create investment in outcome without some version of a person at stake.

**Gap 4: Sources older than 12 months.** The Holders series analysis and most epistolary fiction scholarship I accessed is from 2014-2022. The foundational principles are well-established, but specific craft advice on audio-only application is thin and may not reflect current best practices. The S3F framework (2025) is the only genuinely recent theoretical contribution. The Jane Friedman subtext article is undated but the techniques are stable.

## Synthesis

### The Core Principle All These Works Share

Every project in this research that makes document accumulation feel like a story rather than a collection does one thing: **it establishes, early and specifically, something that is not yet resolved and cannot be ignored.**

MFPNP: the superorganism is going to wake up. We don't know when.
SCP: the anomaly cannot be fully contained. Something is going to happen.
Dracula: someone is hunting Mina and the others don't know it yet.
The Mandela Catalogue: something has already replaced some people around you, and you cannot tell which ones.
The Holders: if you make a single mistake in this procedure, something terrible happens. You are about to make that procedure.

The documents don't create the story. **The unresolved situation creates the story. The documents are the evidence that a situation exists and is unresolved.** This is the distinction between essay (documents as support for a thesis) and narrative (documents as evidence of something that has not yet ended).

### The Five Structural Principles for Single-Episode Application

These emerge from cross-referencing all the reference works against the specific constraints of 60-90 minute TTS single-narrator audio:

**Principle 1: The Episode Needs a Situation, Not a Topic.**
Current episodes have topics (Tesla's patents, the God Particle, the Bell). Narrative requires a situation: *someone was at risk and something was at stake and we don't yet know what happened to them.* The documents are the evidence trail of that situation. This does not require a POV character making discoveries — it requires that at least one document in the episode be narrated by someone who was inside an event that had not yet resolved when they wrote it.

**Principle 2: Documents Must Be Ignorant of Each Other.**
The key mechanical insight from the epistolary tradition: each document is written by someone who does not have access to the other documents in the sequence. The 1899 laboratory notebook does not know what the 1943 Army memo will say. The listener, hearing both, connects them. This connection is the story event — it happens in the listener's mind, not in the narrator's synthesis. The narrator's job is to present documents, not to explain them.

**Principle 3: Format Is Register, Register Is Emotional Distance, Distance Is Escalation.**
The sequence of document formats should move from maximum institutional distance (memo, report, official record) to minimum institutional distance (diary, private letter, handwritten note, testimony given under duress). Each format shift is an emotional step closer to the event. When the episode reaches its most personal document, the listener should feel they are standing next to someone at the moment of revelation.

**Principle 4: Strategic Absence Is More Powerful Than Description.**
Every document should contain one specific, motivated gap — something that *should* be in this document but isn't. The gap must be precise enough that the listener knows what is missing and can supply the worst version of what might fill it. "Pages 112-118 of the Colorado Springs notebook are absent" is a gap. "We do not know what Tesla discovered" is not.

**Principle 5: The Final Document Must Change What the First Document Meant.**
This is the structural requirement for revelation arc. The listener should finish the episode and understand the first document differently than they did when they heard it at the beginning. This is not a twist — it is the retrospective illumination that comes from accumulation. Dracula's opening chapter (Jonathan's travel diary, charming and naive) means something completely different after you have read the whole novel. In a single episode, this means the last document must recontextualize the first.

### Practical Template for Document Sequencing in a 90-Minute Episode

Based on the structural principles above and the evidence from all reference works, the following document arc is supported:

**Opening (0-10 minutes): The Official Record**
Institutional document in the most distanced register possible. Establishes what is publicly known. Contains at least one specific gap or anomaly — something that should be in this document but isn't, or something the institution treats as routine that is not routine. Does not explain the anomaly. Ends without resolution.

**First Complication (10-25 minutes): The Private Account**
A personal document (diary, letter, field notes) from someone inside the events the official record describes. This person does not know what the official record said. They saw something the official record failed to mention or classified differently. Introduce the Unresolved Situation here: someone is at risk, something is happening, and it has not ended. The listener now knows more than any single document.

**Second Complication (25-45 minutes): The Expert Witness**
A technical, scientific, or professional document (engineering report, academic analysis, testimony) that corroborates the private account through a completely different lens — and in doing so reveals that the official record was deliberately incomplete. The expert document should feel like an inadvertent confession: it was trying to explain one thing but its data describes another.

**The Silence (45-55 minutes): The Missing Document**
The narrator explicitly identifies a document that should exist but does not. The precise framing of what is missing — the date, the origin, the expected content — is the horror. This is the episode's still point. Maximum dread comes from silence, not description.

**Resolution (55-75 minutes): The Late Document**
A document from significantly later than the others, written by someone who has partial knowledge of what happened. This person understands more than the earlier writers, but their understanding is still incomplete — they are reaching toward the truth from a different angle. The gap between what they know and what the listener now knows is the dramatic irony. This document changes what the opening document meant.

**Closing (75-90 minutes): The Listener's Synthesis**
The narrator does not synthesize. Instead: the narrator presents the final piece — the document that the listener now understands fully, even though the writer did not. This is the revelation. It should be short. It should be quiet. It should not explain itself. The listener has assembled the truth. The document simply confirms that something unresolved remains.
