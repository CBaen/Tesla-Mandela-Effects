# Research Brief: Narrative Structure for Long-Form Audio Docufiction

## Date: 2026-03-16
## Project: Tesla Mandela Effects

### Problem Statement
We have a docufiction podcast series producing 10,000-13,000 word episodes (~60-90 minutes TTS audio) for YouTube. The episodes are well-written but structured as informational essays. They present evidence and conclusions but have no narrative throughline, no character making discoveries, no story progression. They feel like documentaries, not movies. The creator listened to Episode 001 and found it boring despite the prose quality — it was information without story. For episodes this long, the audience needs a reason to keep listening beyond "this is interesting." They need "what happens next."

### Expected Outcome
A clear understanding of narrative structures that work for long-form audio at the 60-90 minute range, specifically for documentary-style content that blends real history with fictional elements. The output should give us concrete structural templates we can encode into a Production Guide that tells AI writers HOW to build narrative into each episode — not just what facts to present, but how to create a story that carries the listener through 90 minutes.

### Current State
- 10 episodes production-ready, all structured as essays/investigations
- Strong prose quality, strong research depth, weak narrative
- Audit scores: Thesis delivery avg 4.6/10, Dread avg 4.2/10, Pacing avg 5.7/10
- Best episodes (003, 010) use structural conceits (CERN timeline, trial format) that approach narrative without fully achieving it
- Old scripts (archived) had character drama (Dane Tesla POV) and a cosmic horror entity (The Grid) as direct sensory presence — these had narrative but were abandoned when the series pivoted to standalone episodes
- Series targets conspiracy theory and history buff audiences on YouTube
- Episodes must be standalone — no cross-episode references, every episode is entry point zero
- The series blends real Tesla history with fabricated sources and a background mythology about a geometric intelligence (The Grid) that entered through Tesla's birth

### Project Direction
- Target: 200+ episodes
- Background mythology (The Grid) should surface in ~30% of episodes indirectly, ~10% directly — never in every episode
- Anti-cloning system tracks 7 dimensions to prevent repetition at scale
- TTS output via ElevenLabs Eleven Turbo v2 — no music, no sound effects, voice only
- The audience is co-investigators, not students — condescension kills retention

### Constraints
- Voice-only format: no music, no sound effects, no multiple voice actors. Single narrator TTS.
- Standalone episodes: no cross-episode continuity requirements
- Fabricated sources must sound real but never attribute false quotes to real people
- Episodes must be 10,000-13,000 words
- The writing must maintain documentary credibility while building dread
- No markdown in output — pure prose, TTS-ready

### Destructive Boundaries
- Do NOT recommend abandoning the standalone format
- Do NOT recommend adding music or sound effects (TTS constraint)
- Do NOT recommend reducing episode length
- Do NOT recommend changing the Tesla/Mandela Effect subject matter

### Research Angles
1. **Documentary Narrative Structures** — How do shows like Serial, S-Town, Casefile, Fall of Civilizations, and Radiolab create story within documentary? What structural templates do they use? Focus on single-narrator, long-form formats.
2. **Found-Document Worldbuilding** — How does Mystery Flesh Pit National Park, SCP Foundation, House of Leaves, and similar projects create narrative through documents alone? What makes accumulated documents feel like a story rather than a collection?
3. **Investigation/Discovery Narrator** — What is the technique where a narrator discovers things in real-time alongside the listener rather than presenting pre-researched conclusions? How does this work in single-narrator audio? What makes the difference between "here's what I found" (essay) and "come with me as I find this" (story)?
4. **Pacing and Tension Architecture for 60-90 Minute Audio** — What specific structural techniques keep listeners engaged at this length? Story beats, revelation timing, tension/release cycles, the role of unanswered questions as narrative propulsion, the mid-episode re-engagement problem.

### Team Size: 4
Four distinct angles that don't overlap significantly. Each requires different source material and expertise.

### Failed Approaches
- Pure informational essay structure (current 10 episodes — well-written but boring at length)
- Character drama with POV character (old scripts — had narrative but abandoned when series went standalone)
- Trial/structural conceit (Episode 010 — closest to working, but still more essay than story)
