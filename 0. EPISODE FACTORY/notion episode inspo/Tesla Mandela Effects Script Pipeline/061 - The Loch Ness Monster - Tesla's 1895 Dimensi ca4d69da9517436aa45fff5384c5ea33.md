Tesla's Journey: ## Motivation

By 1895, Tesla had achieved fame with alternating current but felt constrained by commercial physics. His true passion was fundamental reality: what principles governed existence at its deepest levels? His experiments with high-frequency electromagnetic fields had revealed anomalies—moments where matter seemed to resonate with something beyond normal spacetime. He theorized that parallel universes existed in quantum superposition, separated by dimensional barriers that electromagnetic resonance could breach. His motivation wasn't commercial; it was proving that reality contained dimensions physics hadn't discovered. If he could open a portal to parallel Earth, he'd demonstrate that consensus reality was far more complex than science acknowledged.

## Process

Tesla spent 1893-1895 developing dimensional resonance theory. His framework suggested that focused electromagnetic oscillation at precisely calculated frequencies could create localized dimensional permeability—essentially vibrating dimensional barriers until they became temporarily transparent. The challenge was scale: small effects were easy in the laboratory, but creating portal large enough to observe required enormous power and perfect resonance conditions.

He needed remote testing location. Dimensional portal failure could be catastrophic—uncontrolled breach might create permanent contamination pathway. Deep water was ideal: submerged apparatus would be isolated, water would dampen uncontrolled resonance spread, and remote location would minimize human exposure. Loch Ness met all criteria.

July 1895: Tesla transported equipment to Scotland, established covert laboratory, and submerged his dimensional resonance generator at loch's deepest point. The apparatus used 24 synchronized Tesla coils operating at precisely calculated frequencies (approximately 150-180 MHz) to create a resonance bubble where dimensional barriers weakened. Additional components included electromagnetic field directors to shape the portal and sensing equipment to detect dimensional breakthrough.

July 22, 1895, 3:17 AM: Portal activation. The water within 3-meter radius became translucent, showing landscape from parallel Earth. Tesla observed for 72 hours, documenting everything. The alternate Earth was recognizable but different—similar geology, different evolutionary outcomes. Aquatic fauna resembled Earth's prehistoric creatures but evolved under different selection pressures.

July 25: Attempted closure. His resonance nullification system failed. The portal had achieved self-sustaining oscillation, drawing energy from dimensional flux itself rather than external power. It collapsed to ~0.01% capacity but wouldn't close completely.

## Realization

By August 1895, after three weeks of failed shutdown attempts, Tesla realized the portal was permanent. He'd created a wound in dimensional fabric that kept reopening. His theoretical framework hadn't predicted self-sustaining resonance. The dimensional barrier, once breached, wanted to remain open—possibly because energy gradients between parallel universes created pressure toward equilibrium.

Worse: he couldn't safely retrieve the apparatus. Approaching it during resonance spikes caused physiological effects—disorientation, temporal distortion sensations, nausea. The portal was generating dimensional instability that made nearby spacetime unreliable.

He realized he'd created a permanent dimensional leak that he couldn't control, couldn't close, and couldn't safely approach. His greatest achievement was also his greatest failure—proving dimensional travel was possible by creating dimensional breach he couldn't repair.

Character 4 - Ordinary Person: ## Full Name
Alexander MacDonald

## Role
Local fisherman and gillie (fishing guide), age 32 in 1895, who assisted Tesla during Loch Ness portal installation

## Background
Highlands native with intimate knowledge of Loch Ness geography, weather patterns, and boat operation. Practical, skeptical of superstition, economically dependent on seasonal fishing and guiding tourists.

## Personal Stakes
Alexander was hired by Tesla in July 1895 to provide boats, local knowledge, and discretion. Paid extraordinarily well to ask no questions. He witnessed the entire portal installation and activation, seeing creatures from parallel Earth emerge through dimensional gateway. He faced impossible choice: report what he'd seen to authorities (risking loss of Tesla's payment and being labeled insane) or keep silent and preserve livelihood.

## Connection to Discovery
Alexander chose pragmatic silence. Tesla paid him annual stipend until 1943 to suppress sightings and discourage investigation. Alexander guided investigators away from the portal site, redirected boats to different areas, and quietly dismissed locals' reports. His cooperation kept the portal secret for 38 years. After Tesla's death and payment cessation in 1943, Alexander gradually allowed sightings to become public, no longer financially motivated to suppress them.

## Character Arc
Began as practical Highlander who viewed local superstitions as tourism entertainment. Witnessing dimensional portal forced him to accept that impossible things were real, hidden beneath familiar waters. Spent 48 years (1895-1943) protecting Tesla's secret for payment while watching dimensional fauna occasionally appear. After Tesla's death, he participated in 'Loch Ness Monster' publicity, knowing truth was far stranger than folklore suggested. Died 1951, age 88, taking detailed knowledge of portal location to his grave (though his journals, discovered 2015, provided crucial evidence). His arc: from skeptical guide to paid guardian of dimensional secret to late-life participant in monster mythology that concealed what he'd witnessed.
