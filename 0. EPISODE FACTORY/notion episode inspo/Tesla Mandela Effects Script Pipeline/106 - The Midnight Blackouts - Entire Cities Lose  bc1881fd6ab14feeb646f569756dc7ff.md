# 106 - The Midnight Blackouts - Entire Cities Lose Power at Exactly Midnight

Alteration Type: Reality Glitch
Date of Occurrence: July 1, 1921
Tesla's Age at Event: 65
Cover Story: Midnight blackouts are equipment failures, maintenance issues, or coordinated cyber attacks. Power companies investigating. Solutions being implemented. Normal infrastructure problems.
Conspiracy Theory: Tesla deliberately designed midnight blackouts into his wireless power system. During these brief shutdown periods, the system performed critical maintenance functions - discharging accumulated electrical stress, recalibrating resonance frequencies, and synchronizing all nodes across the network. But he also used the blackout periods for another purpose: testing whether systematic power interruptions could be coordinated across massive geographic areas for strategic purposes. His notes describe the midnight blackouts as proof-of-concept for controlled, network-wide power management - demonstrating that an interconnected electrical system could be centrally orchestrated. The military immediately grasped the implications: coordinated power delivery could also mean coordinated power denial. Tesla's midnight blackouts pioneered the concept of infrastructure as a centrally controlled strategic asset.
Episode Hook: July 1, 1921: Cities across Tesla's experimental power network experience simultaneous midnight blackouts. Initially assumed coincidental, the pattern repeats nightly. Engineers investigating discover all wireless stations automatically entering maintenance mode at midnight - a coordinated shutdown Tesla programmed into the system. His design notes reveal this was intentional: 'Network requires synchronized rest cycle. Like biological systems. Midnight chosen as minimum usage period. Brief darkness allows system regeneration.' The military examines his work, recognizing he's demonstrated centralized control over distributed electrical infrastructure.
Evidence It Was Different: Engineering reports from July 1921 document the midnight blackout pattern across multiple cities. Tesla's wireless station design specifications include automatic midnight maintenance cycles. His correspondence defends the blackouts as necessary for network health. Military intelligence files from 1921 reference Tesla's 'demonstration of coordinated infrastructure control.' Modern power grid management uses sophisticated synchronization and maintenance protocols Tesla pioneered with his midnight blackout system.
What Changed: July 1921: Multiple cities on Tesla's experimental wireless power grid report simultaneous power failures occurring at exactly midnight local time, lasting 2-4 minutes. The pattern repeats nightly for two weeks. Engineers trace the failures to Tesla's prototype wireless transmission stations automatically synchronizing their maintenance cycles - shutting down at midnight for system recalibration, then restarting. The synchronization is unintentional, caused by all stations using identical timing mechanisms. Cities experience mysterious blackouts because Tesla's distributed network is operating as coordinated system, creating systematic midnight power interruptions.
The Twist: Tesla's final midnight blackout note, July 1921: 'Military requests continuation of blackout testing. They see strategic applications: coordinated power denial as warfare tool. This was never my intent. Blackouts were network maintenance, not weapon demonstration. Have discontinued wireless experiments. They want infrastructure as strategic asset - controlled from center, denied to adversary at will. This inverts my vision. I sought universal power access, freely available like air. They see controlled access, weaponized infrastructure, power as leverage. Cannot continue project if outcome is centralized control rather than distributed abundance. Network remains theoretical triumph. But implementation requires trust I no longer possess. Midnight blackouts proved concept. Also proved danger of trusting technology to institutions that think in terms of control rather than liberation.'
