Tesla's Journey: The concept of electromagnetic propulsion had occupied Tesla's mind since his earliest work with alternating current. If electricity could move through wires without physical contact, if wireless transmission could carry energy across vast distances, then perhaps electromagnetic fields could move physical objects through space itself. The idea seemed fantastical even by Tesla's standards, but the mathematics suggested possibilities that conventional physics dismissed too quickly.

By the late 1920s, Tesla had developed theoretical frameworks for what he called 'spacetime manipulation propulsion.' The principle was elegant: rather than pushing against something to move forward, the craft would modify the curvature of spacetime itself, essentially falling in the desired direction while the universe reshaped around it. Acceleration would be instantaneous from the craft's perspective, limited only by the electromagnetic field strength that could be generated.

The initial calculations were promising. Tesla filled notebooks with equations describing field configurations, power requirements, and expected performance characteristics. A spacecraft using his principles could reach the Moon in hours, Mars in days, the outer planets in weeks. The speed of light remained absolute, but practical interplanetary travel became theoretically achievable with technology that, while advanced, wasn't fundamentally impossible.

Then Tesla encountered the temporal problem.

In 1931, while refining his equations for a lecture he never delivered, Tesla discovered that spacetime manipulation produced an unavoidable side effect. The same field configurations that curved space also curved time. At low intensities, this produced negligible effects—fractions of seconds gained or lost that would never be noticed. But at the power levels required for practical propulsion, the temporal distortions became severe. His calculations showed that under certain conditions, closed timelike curves would form—loops where effect could precede cause, where time itself would fold back on itself.

The implications horrified him. A spacecraft entering such a loop would experience normal time from its internal perspective, but from an external viewpoint, it would repeat the same sequence endlessly. Launch, flight, whatever event terminated the journey—and then back to launch, flight, termination, infinitely. The occupants would be conscious throughout, experiencing the loop as real time while trapped in causality that had closed around them like a prison.

Tesla spent months attempting to solve the problem. He tried different field configurations, alternative power sequencing, modified geometric arrangements. Nothing worked. The temporal loops weren't a flaw in his specific design—they were an inherent consequence of spacetime manipulation above certain thresholds. Any propulsion system powerful enough for practical use would risk trapping its occupants in eternal repetition.

His warnings were explicit. In notes from 1933, Tesla wrote: 'The electromagnetic propulsion device must never be constructed for human transport without first solving the causality loop problem. Consciousness trapped in closed timelike curves would experience infinite duration of whatever sequence initiated the loop. If that sequence includes suffering—injury, fear, death—the subject would experience that suffering eternally. This is not metaphorical. The mathematics are unambiguous. To launch humans using this technology without temporal safeguards is to condemn them to torture without end.'

Tesla never solved the problem. His final years were consumed by other projects—the death ray, wireless power transmission, earthquake prevention—while the propulsion research remained deliberately incomplete. He hoped that by leaving the temporal safeguards unsolved, he would discourage anyone from attempting practical application. The technology was too dangerous in its current state. Better to wait decades or centuries for proper understanding than to trap humans in temporal hell.
