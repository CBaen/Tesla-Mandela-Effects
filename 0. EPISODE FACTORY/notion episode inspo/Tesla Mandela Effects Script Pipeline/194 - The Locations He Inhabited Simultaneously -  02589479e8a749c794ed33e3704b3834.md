The priest documented their conversation in detail—theological questions about consciousness and soul that only Tesla would ask. All three interactions occurred simultaneously in buildings 200 meters apart.

Witness testimony is consistent and credible: 47 students, 3 teachers, and 1 priest independently confirmed Tesla's presence. He wasn't moving rapidly between locations—he was genuinely present in all three places, conducting separate conversations, demonstrating unique knowledge in each context. His consciousness had achieved quantum superposition at macroscopic human scale.

Physical evidence remains: written work from all three simultaneous locations shows Tesla's handwriting, problem-solving approach, and personal knowledge. Signatures match across samples. The mathematics work, physics work, and theological discussion all display Tesla's distinctive intellectual patterns. Not imposters—actual Tesla consciousness distributed across multiple physical instantiations.

Character 4 - Ordinary Person: ## Full Name
Ana Babić

## Role
Fellow student, age 14 in 1871

## Background
Merchant's daughter, one of few female students allowed at Royal Gymnasium. Practical, observant, kept detailed diary that became crucial evidence of bilocation events.

## Personal Stakes
Ana's academic standing depended on faculty respect. Supporting Tesla's bilocation claims risked being labeled as gullible or complicit in fraud. Her father pressured her to recant testimony to protect family reputation.

But she'd witnessed the impossible directly: saw Tesla in hallway walking toward mathematics class, then immediately encountered him coming from opposite direction from physics class. Both Teslas acknowledged her by name. She couldn't deny what she'd seen.

## Connection to Discovery
Ana's diary provided timestamped evidence. She documented daily observations with specific times and locations. Her October 14, 1871 entry: "9:03 AM - Passed Tesla ascending stairs to Professor Horvat's class. 9:05 AM - Encountered Tesla on first floor emerging from Professor Dragić's class. Same clothing, same books, acknowledged me both times. Impossible but true."

Her systematic documentation convinced skeptics because she had no reason to fabricate entries predating the controversy. She'd been recording for personal purposes, not knowing her diary would become evidence.

## Character Arc
Beginning: Conventional student who trusted sensory perception and believed in physical law's predictability. "What you see is what exists."

Transformation: Witnessing Tesla's verified bilocation forced her to accept that reality operated by principles she didn't understand. Struggled with cognitive dissonance but chose honesty over comfort. Her diary entries show evolution from confusion ("I must be mistaken") to acceptance ("This is happening. I don't understand it, but I'm recording it faithfully").

End: Became journalist (1880s-1920s), specializing in scientific anomalies and unexplained phenomena. Her bilocation experience taught her that dismissing evidence because it contradicts theory is bad epistemology. Published extensively on the importance of observation over assumption. Maintained correspondence with Tesla for 45 years. Her 1916 memoir chapter on the bilocation events provided most detailed firsthand account. She died 1922, age 65, having built career on principle she learned at 14: document what you observe, even when you can't explain it.
