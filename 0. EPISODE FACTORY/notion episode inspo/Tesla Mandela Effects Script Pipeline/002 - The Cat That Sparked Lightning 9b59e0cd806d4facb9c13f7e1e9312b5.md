# 002 - The Cat That Sparked Lightning

Alteration Type: Reality Glitch
Date of Occurrence: January 1, 1862
Tesla's Age at Event: 6
Cover Story: Static electricity from winter air and cat fur is a normal and well-understood physical phenomenon. When humidity is low and materials rub together, electrons transfer between surfaces, creating visible sparks. Children's imaginations are highly suggestible, and a six-year-old witnessing dramatic electrical sparks would naturally create narratives around the experience. Psychologists note that formative childhood experiences often become exaggerated in memory, especially when the individual later becomes famous. Tesla's lifelong interest in electricity likely began with this memorable static electricity experience, but attributing any supernatural communication or 'programming' to it represents retroactive mythologizing of a simple natural event.
Conspiracy Theory: Something used the cat as a transmission device, the electrical discharge as the medium to program a six-year-old's brain and set him on a path spanning his lifetime. The cat didn't speak—something spoke through the cat, using electricity as a carrier wave. Tesla spent the next 60 years chasing that blue light, building machines designed to produce it, his entire existence devoted to capturing the phenomenon he witnessed for mere seconds on a winter night. Every major invention—the alternating current motor, the Tesla coil, wireless power transmission—was an attempt to recreate and understand that blue light. The question that haunted Tesla his entire life: was it inspiration showing him his destiny, or was it programming, installing a mission he had no choice but to fulfill?
Episode Hook: A researcher studying Tesla's early life interviews descendants of Smiljan families and discovers a disturbing pattern. Multiple witnesses over decades report cats in the area producing the same blue light effect—sparking with unusual intensity, eyes glowing. Modern electromagnetic analysis shows the Smiljan region has persistent EM anomalies where cats act as natural conductors for an unknown frequency. Tesla's childhood experience wasn't unique—it was first documented contact with a phenomenon that specifically targets cats as transmission devices. The blue wavelength that appears in all Tesla's later work matches exactly the frequency these Smiljan cats conduct. He didn't invent that color of electricity—he spent 60 years recreating the signal that programmed him at age six.
