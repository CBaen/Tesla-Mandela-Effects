Cover Story: The Tunguska event of June 30, 1908 was a natural airburst explosion caused by a meteor or comet fragment entering Earth's atmosphere and disintegrating 5-10 kilometers above the surface. Seismic readings, atmospheric pressure waves recorded globally, and eyewitness accounts of a fireball all support the meteor hypothesis. Trees flattened in radial pattern match airburst shockwave physics. No meteor fragments were recovered because the object completely vaporized during atmospheric entry. Subsequent expeditions found microscopic silicate and magnetite spherules consistent with extraterrestrial origin. Claims about Tesla's involvement are chronologically impossible—he had no particle beam weapon in 1908 and no means of targeting Siberia from New York. The conspiracy theory conflates Tesla's later Teleforce concepts with an unrelated natural disaster, misattributing temporal causation to explain an adequately understood meteor impact.

Subsequent Teleforce tests between 1915-1917 created additional temporal displacement events, with energy beams emerging at random points in Earth's past and future. Some beams may still be traveling through time, yet to emerge. Tesla immediately ceased Teleforce development and destroyed all weaponized particle beam research, horrified by the temporal damage he'd inflicted. But military observers had witnessed the tests and recovered fragments of Tesla's temporal displacement calculations. Modern directed-energy weapons research secretly incorporates Tesla's temporal mechanics, attempting to weaponize causality violations.

Character 4 - Ordinary Person: ## Full Name
Dmitri Volkov (Siberian hunter and trader, age 34 in 1908)

## Role
Local Evenki indigenous hunter living 65 kilometers from Tunguska epicenter

## Background
Traditional hunter familiar with Siberian wilderness. No formal education but deep practical knowledge of forest ecology and animal behavior. Trusted by community as reliable observer.

## Personal Stakes
Morning of June 30, 1908, Dmitri was hunting when he saw vertical column of blue-white light appear from clear sky, miles away. Described it as 'pillar of fire that came from nowhere, pointing from sky to ground like giant finger.' Watched for approximately 30 seconds before massive explosion knocked him unconscious. Woke hours later, forest around him flattened, trees pointing away from light pillar location. Lost three family members in fires following explosion. Spent remaining 21 years (1908-1929) trying to understand what he'd seen, describing it to every scientist who visited region.

## Connection to Discovery
His eyewitness testimony describing vertical light beam rather than meteor fireball provided crucial evidence contradicting natural impact hypothesis. His detailed directional descriptions helped modern researchers triangulate particle beam origin point. Most significantly, he drew diagrams showing light appearing from empty sky, not from horizon—impossible for meteor but consistent with beam emerging from temporal rift.

## Character Arc
Beginning: Simple hunter who understood natural world through traditional knowledge. Believed natural phenomena had natural explanations.

Transformation: Witnessing impossible vertical light beam from clear sky broke his understanding of natural causation. Spent two decades trying to reconcile what he saw with any natural explanation. Failed. Came to believe he'd witnessed something outside natural world—"fire that came from tomorrow" as he described it in 1927 interview.

End: Died 1929 still haunted by the impossible sight. His final testimony to Soviet researcher: 'I know what I saw. Light from sky, not from above but from nowhere. It appeared already moving, like it had been traveling before it arrived. Scientists say meteor. I say something else. Something that broke through from somewhere.'
