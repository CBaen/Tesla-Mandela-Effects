### CHECK 1 — Tesla at the Hotel St. Regis in 1922

**Claim in brief:** Tesla was at the Hotel St. Regis (Fifth Avenue and Fifty-fifth Street, New York) during the pigeon period (approximately 1918–1922). He was evicted from the St. Regis in 1922 for non-payment of debts.

**Why this matters:** Episode 009 of this series already established Tesla's hotel residencies. That episode noted Tesla was at the St. Regis in 1922. If those facts are wrong in this episode, an internal continuity failure results. Additionally, Episode 001's v2 brief states Tesla died in Room 3327 of the Hotel New Yorker — writing agents must know Tesla was NOT at the New Yorker in 1922.

**Verify:**
- Was Tesla resident at the Hotel St. Regis during the pigeon period? (Seifer's "Wizard" and Carlson's "Tesla: Inventor of the Electrical Age" are the primary sources to check)
- When exactly was Tesla evicted from the St. Regis? Was it 1922?
- What hotel(s) did Tesla live in between the St. Regis and the New Yorker?
- The Hotel New Yorker: confirm its opening date. The brief states it opened in 1930. Is this correct?
- Confirm: Tesla lived at the New Yorker from 1933 until his death in January 1943?

**Source to check:** Seifer, Marc. "Wizard: The Life and Times of Nikola Tesla." 1996. Birch Lane Press. / Carlson, W. Bernard. "Tesla: Inventor of the Electrical Age." 2013. Princeton University Press.

### CHECK 2 — O'Neill's Documentation of the White Pigeon

**Claim in brief:** John J. O'Neill's "Prodigal Genius: The Life of Nikola Tesla" (1944, Ives Washburn, New York) documents:
- A specific white pigeon with gray-tipped wings
- Tesla's statement that the pigeon "understood him"
- A beam of light from the pigeon's eyes at the moment of death
- Tesla's statement that "something went out of my life" when the pigeon died
- Tesla's statement that he knew his life's work was finished

**Why this matters:** The brief instructs writing agents to treat all these as reported speech via O'Neill, not as direct quotes in quotation marks — because the exact phrasing from O'Neill's text needs to be confirmed before deciding how to attribute it. If any of these details appear in O'Neill as direct quotes from Tesla, the Quote Rule applies: they must be exact verified words.

**Verify:**
- Does O'Neill's "Prodigal Genius" (1944) contain all five of the above claims?
- What are the exact page numbers and exact language O'Neill uses for each claim?
- Are Tesla's words given in quotation marks in O'Neill's text, or paraphrased?
- The pigeon's description: does O'Neill specifically say "gray-tipped wings"? Or is the description different?
- The beam of light: does O'Neill describe it as Tesla's account, or does O'Neill describe it differently?
- Approximately when does O'Neill place the pigeon's death? The brief says "approximately 1922." Is this what O'Neill records?

**Source to check:** O'Neill, John J. "Prodigal Genius: The Life of Nikola Tesla." 1944. Ives Washburn, New York. (Available via Internet Archive, Project Gutenberg, or library systems.)

### CHECK 3 — Pigeon Magnetoreception Science

**Claim in brief:** Pigeons navigate using magnetite crystals in their beaks and inner ears, allowing them to sense electromagnetic fields. Mora et al., Nature, 2004, is cited as a key reference.

**Why this matters:** The electromagnetic navigation claim is the scientific bridge between the pigeon and Tesla's experiments. If the science is wrong, the episode's central mechanism fails.

**Verify:**
- Is Mora et al. (2004) a real publication in Nature? What does it specifically demonstrate about pigeon magnetoreception?
- Is the claim about magnetite crystals in beaks and inner ears currently the accepted scientific account, or has it been substantially revised since 2004?
- The cryptochrome / quantum effects alternative: is this a genuine competing hypothesis in current literature?
- Is "magnetite crystals" the accurate term, or should it be "magnetite particles" or "magnetite-based receptors"?
- The Schumann resonance: confirm it is approximately 7.83 hertz, conventionally rounded to 8 Hz. Confirm Schumann first described it in 1952.
- Confirm: Tesla's Colorado Springs experiments (1899–1900) identified the Earth's resonant frequency. Is the identification of this frequency as approximately 8 Hz documented in Tesla's own records or in reliable biographies?

**Source to check:** Mora, C.V., Davison, M., Wild, J.M., and Walker, M.M. "Magnetoreception and its trigeminal mediation in the homing pigeon." Nature 432, 508–511 (2004). / Schumann, W.O. (1952) — verify publication details. / Carlson, W. Bernard. "Tesla: Inventor of the Electrical Age." 2013.
