## SECTION A — Exposition Facts

### A1 — Fair dates
**Claim:** The fair ran from May 1, 1893, to October 30, 1893.
**Source cited:** Erik Larson, "The Devil in the White City" (2003); official Exposition records at the Chicago History Museum.
**Verify:** Confirm opening date (May 1, 1893) and closing date (October 30, 1893). Note if there is any ambiguity about the closing date (some sources say October 28 or 30).
**If unverifiable:** Use "May through October, eighteen ninety-three" without specific dates.

### A2 — Exposition acreage
**Claim:** 690 acres of former lakefront swamp and parkland in Jackson Park, Chicago.
**Source cited:** Larson, "The Devil in the White City"; Bancroft, "The Book of the Fair" (1893).
**Verify:** Confirm 690 acres as the correct total. Note if the figure refers to total grounds or developed grounds only.
**If unverifiable:** Omit the specific acreage and use qualitative description.

### A3 — Attendance
**Claim:** Approximately 27 million visitors.
**Source cited:** Standard reference; Larson.
**Verify:** Confirm the attendance figure is approximately 27 million. Note if this is total paid admissions or a different count.
**If unverifiable:** Use "tens of millions of visitors" without a specific number.

### A4 — Groundbreaking date
**Claim:** Groundbreaking took place in fall 1890.
**Source cited:** Larson; official Exposition records.
**Verify:** Confirm fall 1890 as the general period of groundbreaking. The brief previously cited October 28, 1890 as the specific date but noted this was unverifiable online. Confirm whether a specific date is documentable. If not, use "fall, eighteen ninety."
**If unverifiable:** Use "fall, eighteen ninety" without a specific date.

### A5 — Staff material composition
**Claim:** "Staff" was a composite of plaster of Paris, cement, glycerin, and fibrous binders including jute fiber and dextrin.
**Source cited:** Chicago History Museum records; The Inland Architect, 1892-1893.
**Verify:** Confirm the composition is accurately described. Specifically: confirm that dextrin was used (it appears in some accounts and not others). Confirm jute fiber was a standard binder.
**If dextrin is unverifiable:** Omit dextrin and use "fibrous binders" without specifying.

### A6 — Number of lamps
**Claim:** The brief notes sources vary between 100,000 and 250,000 lamps. The brief recommends using the more conservative figure.
**Verify:** Find the source basis for both figures. The 100,000 figure and the 250,000 figure appear to describe different things (individual incandescent lamps vs. total light sources including arc lamps). Clarify what each figure counts.
**For the episode:** Use the better-supported figure with its specific attribution, noting any uncertainty. Do not blend the two figures.

## SECTION B — Tesla and Westinghouse

### B1 — Westinghouse won the lighting contract
**Claim:** Nikola Tesla and the Westinghouse Electric Company won the contract to light the 1893 World's Columbian Exposition.
**Source cited:** W. Bernard Carlson, "Tesla: Inventor of the Electrical Age" (Princeton University Press, 2013).
**Verify:** Confirm Westinghouse won the lighting contract over General Electric. Confirm the contract was for the entire fair's illumination, not a portion of it.
**If unverifiable:** This is well-documented; if for some reason a specific source cannot be cited, it should be presentable as documented historical fact without specific citation in the narration.

### B2 — Westinghouse contract amount
**Claim:** The brief flags this as unverifiable — a specific dollar amount appears in some sources (around $399,000 or $510,000) but the figures conflict.
**Verify:** Look up the Westinghouse contract amount in Carlson's book specifically. Report the figure Carlson gives. If no specific figure can be confirmed, this number should not appear in the episode.
**If unverifiable:** Do not cite a specific dollar amount. The episode does not need one.

### B3 — Tesla's egg-of-Columbus demonstration
**Claim:** Tesla personally demonstrated AC at the Electricity Building, including his egg-of-Columbus demonstration (spinning a copper egg using a rotating magnetic field from a polyphase AC motor).
**Source cited:** Carlson, "Tesla: Inventor of the Electrical Age" (2013); contemporaneous accounts.
**Verify:** Confirm the egg-of-Columbus demonstration occurred at the 1893 World's Fair specifically. Note: some sources place variations of this demonstration at different venues. Confirm it occurred at the Electricity Building.
**If unverifiable for the 1893 fair specifically:** Adjust the claim to "his demonstrations of AC power, including rotating magnetic field displays, at the Electricity Building" without naming the egg specifically.

### B4 — Other Tesla demonstrations
**Claim:** The brief flags Tesla's other demonstrations (wireless gas-discharge lamps, body-current demonstrations) as needing verification specifically for the 1893 fair. Some of these demonstrations are associated with other venues and years.
**Verify:** Which specific demonstrations, beyond the egg-of-Columbus, did Tesla give at the 1893 fair's Electricity Building? What does Carlson's biography document specifically for this venue?
**For the episode:** Use only demonstrations confirmed for the 1893 fair. Do not attribute demonstrations from other venues or years to this event.

## SECTION C — What Was Demolished and What Survived

### C1 — Museum of Science and Industry origin and rebuild
**Claim:** The Museum of Science and Industry building (originally the Palace of Fine Arts) is the only surviving Jackson Park fair building — but it was rebuilt in limestone beginning in the late 1920s and opened in its current form in 1933, replacing the staff exterior with permanent stone. Funding from Julius Rosenwald.
**Source cited:** Museum of Science and Industry official history; Chicago Tribune coverage, 1928-1933.
**Verify:**
- Confirm the Palace of Fine Arts / Museum of Science and Industry is the only surviving principal Jackson Park structure.
- Confirm the rebuild in Indiana limestone: when did construction begin, and when did it open in its current form?
- Confirm Julius Rosenwald's funding role.
- Confirm the original staff exterior was replaced (not simply encased) in the rebuild.
**If dates are imprecise:** Use approximate dates ("the late nineteen twenties" for the rebuild start, "nineteen thirty-three" for the opening if that date is confirmed).

### C2 — Post-fair fires
**Claim:** The fair's buildings were destroyed by fire and demolition: a fire on July 10, 1894 (or July 5 — accounts vary) destroyed the Casino, Music Hall, and other structures.
**Source cited:** Larson; Chicago Tribune contemporaneous coverage.
**Verify:** Confirm the correct date (or dates) of the major post-fair fire(s). The Cold Storage Building fire during the fair (July 10, 1893) is separate — confirm the date of the post-fair fires that destroyed the main buildings.
**If dates conflict:** Use the range "July, eighteen ninety-four" without a specific date.

### C3 — Cold Storage Building fire casualties
**Claim:** The Cold Storage Building fire during the fair (July 10, 1893) killed fifteen people — twelve firefighters and three civilians.
**Source cited:** The brief flags this as verified but should be confirmed before use.
**Verify:** Confirm the casualty count: fifteen dead, twelve firefighters and three civilians. Confirm the date (July 10, 1893, during the fair, not after).
**If casualty count differs:** Use the confirmed figure with its source.

## SECTION D — Chicago Grade Raise

### D1 — Grade raise measurements
**Claim:** Chicago raised its street grade by approximately four to seven feet, with the most intensive work concentrated in the 1850s through 1860s.
**Source cited:** Louis P. Cain, "Sanitation Strategy for a Lakefront Metropolis" (1978); Chicago History Museum records.
**Verify:** Confirm the approximate four-to-seven-foot range. Confirm the 1850s-1860s as the intensive period.
**If measurements vary:** Report the range found and its source. The episode uses "approximately" so some variance is acceptable.

### D2 — Ellis Sylvester Chesbrough
**Claim:** The project was overseen by engineer Ellis Sylvester Chesbrough.
**Source cited:** Cain; Chicago History Museum records.
**Verify:** Confirm Chesbrough's role and full name (Ellis Sylvester Chesbrough).
**If unverifiable:** Use "the city's chief engineer" without the proper name if the name cannot be confirmed.

### D3 — The Briggs House hotel
**Claim:** The Briggs House was famously lifted while guests were inside.
**Source cited:** Standard historical account.
**Verify:** Confirm the Briggs House hotel was raised on jackscrews while occupied. Confirm the hotel's correct name (Briggs House, not Briggs Hotel or another variant).
**If name is wrong:** Use the correct name.

## SECTION E — Attribution Rules Verification

### E1 — Burnham "make no little plans" quote
**Claim:** The brief flags this quote as disputed — it may have been composed after Burnham's death by Willis Polk. The brief says: do not present this as a verified direct quote.
**Verify:** Confirm the attribution dispute. Who is Willis Polk and what is the basis for attributing the quote to him rather than Burnham? When does the quote first appear in print?
**For the episode:** If the quote is used at all, present it as "attributed to Burnham, though its origins are disputed." Do not place it in quotation marks as if it is a verified direct quote.

### E2 — Tesla quotes
**Flag:** Any direct quote attributed to Nikola Tesla in the episode script must be his exact verified words. The brief does not prescribe Tesla quotes for this episode — the episode relies on documented historical facts about Tesla's presence rather than his words. If a writing agent introduces a Tesla quote, it must be verified before delivery.
**Action:** Flag any Tesla direct quote for separate verification against a primary source (Tesla's memoir "My Inventions," O'Neill's "Prodigal Genius," Carlson's biography as a secondary source for documented quotes).
