# Episode 002 — THE WHITE CITY
## Fact-Check Request
### For external verification via web browser before or during writing

All fabricated sources in this episode are clearly labeled as fabricated in the brief. This document lists only the REAL, verifiable claims that must survive a listener Googling them.

## Priority 1 — Verify Before Writing Begins

These facts are structural. If any are wrong, the brief must be amended before writing starts.

### 1.1 Fair Dates and Scale
**Claim:** The World's Columbian Exposition ran from May 1, 1893, to October 30, 1893.
**Claim:** Groundbreaking was October 28, 1890.
**Claim:** The fairgrounds covered 690 acres in Jackson Park, Chicago.
**Claim:** Attendance was approximately 27.5 million.
**Source to check:** Chicago History Museum, Encyclopedia Britannica, or Erik Larson's "The Devil in the White City" (2003).
**What to verify:** Exact opening/closing dates, groundbreaking date, acreage, attendance figure.

### 1.2 Westinghouse Contract Amount
**Claim:** The Westinghouse Electric Company's contract to light the fair was approximately $399,000.
**Claim:** This significantly underbid the General Electric proposal.
**Source to check:** W. Bernard Carlson, "Tesla: Inventor of the Electrical Age" (Princeton University Press, 2013); contemporaneous accounts in Electrical World journal (1893).
**What to verify:** The $399,000 figure. The GE underbid relationship. The contractual relationship between Tesla, Westinghouse, and the fair.

### 1.3 Lamp Count
**Claim:** The Westinghouse-Tesla system illuminated the fair with approximately 100,000 incandescent lamps (some sources say 250,000).
**Source to check:** Carlson biography; contemporaneous accounts.
**What to verify:** Which figure is more defensible. The episode will use the more conservative figure with acknowledgment that accounts vary. Confirm whether 100,000 is documented or whether 250,000 is the primary source.

### 1.4 Tesla's Specific Demonstrations at the Fair
**Claim:** Tesla demonstrated the egg-of-Columbus at the Electricity Building — spinning a copper egg using a rotating magnetic field from a polyphase AC motor.
**Claim:** Tesla passed high-frequency current through his own body to light fluorescent tubes.
**Claim:** Both demonstrations took place at the Electricity Building during the 1893 fair.
**Source to check:** Carlson, "Tesla: Inventor of the Electrical Age" (2013), chapters covering the 1893 period; Electrical World journal, May-October 1893.
**What to verify:** That both demonstrations occurred at the 1893 fair specifically. That the location was the Electricity Building. That these are documented in primary sources, not just popular accounts.

### 1.5 Staff Material Composition
**Claim:** Staff was a composite of plaster of Paris, cement, glycerin, and jute fiber, troweled over wire lath and wooden or steel frames.
**Claim:** Staff allowed rapid construction and detailed decorative surfaces but was not self-supporting — it required the underlying frame.
**Source to check:** Chicago History Museum records; contemporaneous accounts in The Inland Architect (1892-1893); Larson, "The Devil in the White City."
**What to verify:** The specific composition. Whether glycerin is correct (some accounts say other binders). Whether "wire lath" is the right term or whether it was lathe-and-plaster over wood.

### 1.6 Post-Fair Demolition and Fires
**Claim:** A fire on or around July 10, 1894, destroyed multiple fair buildings.
**Claim:** The Cold Storage Building burned during the fair itself on July 10, 1893, killing 17 firefighters.
**Claim:** The vast majority of buildings were demolished or burned between late 1893 and 1894.
**Source to check:** Chicago Tribune contemporaneous coverage, 1893-1894; Larson, "The Devil in the White City"; Chicago History Museum.
**What to verify:** The specific fire dates (July 1894 — multiple fires may have occurred; clarify). The Cold Storage Building fire date and death toll. What "the vast majority" means — is it accurate that almost everything was destroyed?

### 1.7 Surviving Buildings
**Claim:** The only surviving Jackson Park fair building is the Museum of Science and Industry (originally the Palace of Fine Arts).
**Claim:** The Palace of Fine Arts was rebuilt in permanent limestone between 1929 and 1940 with funding from Julius Rosenwald, replacing the staff exterior.
**Claim:** The Art Institute of Chicago building survived but was not on the Jackson Park fairgrounds — it was in Grant Park.
**Source to check:** Museum of Science and Industry official history; Chicago History Museum; Art Institute of Chicago official history.
**What to verify:** Is the Museum of Science and Industry genuinely the only surviving Jackson Park building? Is the rebuild timeline (1929-1940) and Rosenwald funding accurate? Is the Art Institute's location correctly described?

## Priority 2 — Verify During Writing

These facts appear in the episode but are not load-bearing to the central argument. Verify before the relevant section is written.

### 2.1 Chicago Street Grade Raise
**Claim:** Chicago raised its street grade by approximately four to seven feet between approximately 1855 and 1900.
**Claim:** The project was overseen by engineer Ellis Sylvester Chesbrough.
**Claim:** Buildings were raised on jackscrews while occupants were inside.
**Claim:** The Briggs Hotel was famously lifted while guests were inside.
**Source to check:** Louis P. Cain, "Sanitation Strategy for a Lakefront Metropolis" (1978); Chicago History Museum; contemporaneous newspaper accounts.
**What to verify:** The grade raise measurement range (4-7 feet). Chesbrough's role. The jackscrew method. Whether the Briggs Hotel is correctly named as the famous example, or whether it was a different hotel.

### 2.2 Daniel Burnham's Role and the "Make No Little Plans" Quote
**Claim:** Daniel H. Burnham served as Director of Works for the World's Columbian Exposition.
**Claim:** The quote "Make no little plans. They have no magic to stir men's blood" is attributed to Burnham but may have been composed after his death by Willis Polk.
**Source to check:** Charles Moore, "Daniel H. Burnham, Architect: Planner of Cities" (1921); historical attribution research.
**What to verify:** Burnham's specific title at the fair. The disputed attribution of the quote — was it Willis Polk who composed it, or is that attribution also uncertain?

### 2.3 Frederick Law Olmsted's Role
**Claim:** Frederick Law Olmsted oversaw landscape design for the exposition.
**Source to check:** Standard Olmsted biography; Larson, "The Devil in the White City."
**What to verify:** That Olmsted's role was specifically landscape design. His age and health at the time (he was elderly during the project — verify this detail if used).

### 2.4 The Electricity Building's Location and Layout
**Claim:** Tesla's demonstrations took place in the Electricity Building.
**Claim:** The Electricity Building was one of the Court of Honor buildings.
**Source to check:** Historical maps of the fair; Larson; Carlson.
**What to verify:** Whether the Electricity Building was formally considered part of the Court of Honor or adjacent to it. The building's location on the fairgrounds relative to other mentioned structures.

### 2.5 The Manufactures and Liberal Arts Building Scale
**Claim:** The Manufactures and Liberal Arts Building was one of the largest buildings in the world at the time of the fair.
**Source to check:** Larson; historical accounts.
**What to verify:** This claim if used. The building's dimensions. Whether it was, in fact, among the largest buildings then existing.

## Priority 3 — Verify if Used in Writing

These are secondary facts that may or may not be incorporated. Verify only if the writing agent uses them.

### 3.1 The Fair's Anniversary
**Claim:** The fair celebrated the 400th anniversary of Columbus's 1492 voyage.
**What to verify:** That this is correct (1492 + 400 = 1892, not 1893 — the fair was one year late due to construction delays, which may be worth noting or may not).

### 3.2 Hubert Howe Bancroft's "The Book of the Fair"
**Claim:** Bancroft published a contemporaneous account of the fair titled "The Book of the Fair" (1893).
**What to verify:** Publication date and author. This is used as a Layer 1 source citation.

### 3.3 Construction Worker Numbers
If the episode uses specific figures about construction workforce size, verify them. Larson's book contains these figures; use only what is documented there.

### 3.4 The Tesla-Westinghouse AC Demonstration as "Public Debut"
**Claim:** The 1893 World's Fair was the public debut of alternating current.
**What to verify:** Whether this framing is accurate. AC had been demonstrated before 1893 — the Buffalo/Niagara Falls commission came later (1896). The 1893 fair may be more accurately described as the first large-scale public demonstration of AC power for general illumination. Clarify the correct framing.

The "Make no little plans" quote must NOT be presented as a verified direct quote. If used, frame as: attributed to Burnham in contemporaneous accounts, with disputed provenance.

### Quote Usage — Tesla

All four fabricated sources (Coburn's letter, Halsted & Briggs incident report, Halsted Photographic Collection negatives, Alcott's 1904 analysis) are clearly not real. Writing agents must not attach them to real catalog numbers, real archives, or real named researchers. If any archive mentioned needs to be checked:

- Elias Coburn: FICTIONAL. Do not create documentary evidence in real archives.
- R.T. Alcott: FICTIONAL. The Ryerson and Burnham Libraries are real; the Alcott accession materials are not.
- Halsted & Briggs Wrecking: FICTIONAL as described. The Smiley & Sons name variant is noted in the brief as a different company from Smiley Steel in Episode 009 — do not cross-contaminate.
