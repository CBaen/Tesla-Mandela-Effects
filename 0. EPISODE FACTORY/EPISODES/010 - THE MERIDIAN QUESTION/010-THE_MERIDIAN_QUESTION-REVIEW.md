## FACTUAL ISSUES

---

### FINDING 1 — CRITICAL
**Wardenclyffe underground shaft depth: 55 feet vs. 120 feet**

Location: "A shaft driven fifty-five feet into the soil."

What is wrong: The script states the shaft was 55 feet deep. All primary sources give the shaft depth as 120 feet (37 meters), with sixteen iron pipes driven a further 300 feet below the shaft. The 55-foot figure appears nowhere in the established record.

Correct fact: The shaft was 120 feet deep. From it, iron pipes extended another 300 feet into the geological substrate. Tesla himself stated: "the underground work is one of the most expensive parts of the tower. In this system that I have invented it is necessary for the machine to get a grip of the earth."

Source: Wardenclyffe Tower, Wikipedia; Tesla Science Center at Wardenclyffe; geophysical investigation report (THG Geophysics, 2021).

Fix: Change "fifty-five feet" to "one hundred and twenty feet."

---

### FINDING 2 — CRITICAL
**Cognitive maps journal: "Acta Psychologica" is wrong — the paper was published in the Journal of General Psychology**

Location: "The study was published in nineteen eighty-four in the journal Acta Psychologica."

What is wrong: PMID 6512518 (Okabayashi and Glynn, 1984, "Spatial cognition: systematic distortions in cognitive maps") was published in the Journal of General Psychology, volume 111, issue 2, pages 271-279. It was not published in Acta Psychologica. Acta Psychologica is a different journal (Dutch, Elsevier). This is a verifiable error: any listener who searches PubMed for PMID 6512518 will find the Journal of General Psychology, not Acta Psychologica.

Correct fact: The paper was published in the Journal of General Psychology (J Gen Psychol), 1984, 111(2):271-9. Authors: H. Okabayashi and S.M. Glynn.

Source: PubMed, PMID 6512518; Tandfonline.com, "Spatial Cognition: Systematic Distortions in Cognitive Maps: The Journal of General Psychology: Vol 111, No 2."

Fix: Change "the journal Acta Psychologica" to "the Journal of General Psychology."

---

### FINDING 3 — IMPORTANT
**Mercator map date given as "Eighteen sixty-nine" — should be 1569**

Location: "Eighteen sixty-nine. Duisburg, in the Duchy of Cleves."

What is wrong: The Mercator projection was published in 1569, not 1869. The script writes this out as "Eighteen sixty-nine" — which, spoken aloud for TTS, will render as the year 1869. The correct year is 1569, which would be spoken as "Fifteen sixty-nine." Mercator died in 1594; he could not have published anything in 1869. The Brief correctly states 1569.

This is almost certainly a drafting error (a dropped digit) rather than a factual misbelief, but in TTS output the listener will hear a clearly impossible date for a historical figure who died in 1594. It will pull the listener out.

Correct fact: Gerardus Mercator published his world map projection in 1569.

Source: Mercator projection, Wikipedia; Britannica; all primary sources on Mercator.

Fix: Change "Eighteen sixty-nine" to "Fifteen sixty-nine."

### FINDING 4 — IMPORTANT
**South America size comparison: "fifty percent larger than Europe" understates the actual figure**

Location: "South America, in physical reality, is fifty percent larger than Europe."

What is wrong: South America is approximately 17.84 million km². Europe is approximately 10.18 million km². South America is approximately 75-80% larger than Europe — close to double — not 50% larger. The Brief correctly states "more than 50% larger than Europe," which is defensible as a floor. But the script converts the Brief's "more than 50%" to exactly "fifty percent," which actually underrepresents the distortion. The claim is not wrong in direction but is a meaningful understatement that reduces the rhetorical force.

Note: Africa vs. Greenland (fourteen times) is correctly stated. The South America figure is the one to address.

Correct fact: South America is approximately 75-80% larger than Europe, or roughly "nearly double the size."

Source: CIA World Factbook; multiple continent size comparison resources.

Fix: Change "fifty percent larger" to "nearly double the size" or "almost twice as large." This is also more rhetorically consistent with the sentence that follows ("It is nearly twice the size of the United States").

### FINDING 5 — IMPORTANT
**Colorado Springs claim: "It killed the local power station" is an overstatement**

Location: "The lightning he generated was visible from the next county. It killed the local power station."

What is wrong: The claim that Tesla "killed the local power station" overstates what the historical record documents. Tesla's experiments damaged dynamos at a power house approximately six miles away — the dynamos were repeatedly burned out due to high-frequency currents induced in their windings, requiring repair. Some sources mention a brief outage. But the Colorado Springs Electric Company generator was repaired within days and Tesla was ordered to repair it at his own expense. "Killed the local power station" implies permanent or complete destruction, which did not occur. This is a compound overstatement: the power house was not "local" (it was six miles away) and was not "killed" (it was damaged and repaired).

Correct formulation: Tesla's experiments damaged the dynamos at a power house six miles away, burning out their windings with induced high-frequency currents and briefly interrupting the town's power supply.

Source: Tesla Experimental Station, Wikipedia; Tesla's own 1917 account in The Electrical Experimenter; PBS Tesla biography.

Fix: Replace "killed the local power station" with a formulation that acknowledges the damage and temporary disruption without claiming permanent destruction.

## ATTRIBUTION ISSUES

---

### FINDING 6 — CRITICAL
**The Ascension Island Observatory is a real, active institution with accessible historical records — the fabricated log entries are attached to a checkable archive**

Location: "The Royal Observatory on Ascension Island maintained a continuous record of magnetic declination... The declination logs for nineteen oh three through nineteen oh five, reviewed by researchers accessing the Observatory's historical archives..."

What is wrong: The Ascension Island Magnetic Observatory is a real, operating institution managed by the British Geological Survey. Its historical records are held at The National Archives (UK) and through the BGS. The BGS has published papers on its operational history going back to the early 20th century. A curious listener who searches "Ascension Island magnetic observatory historical records" will find the BGS operations page, the National Archives catalogue, and published research papers — none of which will contain the fabricated 1903-1905 anomalous entries the script describes.

The phrase "reviewed by researchers accessing the Observatory's historical archives" compounds the problem: it claims these fabricated records were accessed at a real, named archive. This is a Layer 2 fabrication attached to a Layer 1 institution in a way that breaks the Attribution Rule.

The brief's Source 2 description says the format is a "monitoring station log with handwritten annotation" — which is fine in principle. But the script goes further by naming the real institution and claiming researchers accessed its actual historical archives.

Fix: The institution needs to be genericized. Remove "Royal Observatory on Ascension Island" and replace with a description: "a South Atlantic monitoring station on the longitude of eastern South America." Remove the reference to "researchers accessing the Observatory's historical archives." The geographic specificity of the longitude argument can be preserved without naming a real, checkable archive.

## CRAFT ISSUES

---

### Law 5 Violations

### FINDING 7 — IMPORTANT
**The hippocampus/electromagnetic field claim is presented as established fact without sufficient hedging**

Location: "The place cells in the hippocampus encode spatial position through a mechanism that involves, among other factors, the brain's reading of electromagnetic field orientation. The same mechanism that allows a bird to navigate using the Earth's magnetic field operates in a less precise but structurally analogous way in the mammalian brain."

What is wrong: This is a declarative statement of scientific fact where the science does not support declaration. What is well-documented: hippocampal place cells encode spatial position using visual landmarks, olfactory cues, vestibular input, and boundary distance. What is not established: that mammalian place cells use electromagnetic field orientation as a primary or even significant input. The bird navigation comparison is real — many birds have magnetoreceptors — but the claim that "the same mechanism... operates in a less precise but structurally analogous way in the mammalian brain" is a significant extrapolation. Magnetoreception in mammals is not established to the level the script asserts.

The script needs hedging here: "what remains underexplored is whether" or "what has not been ruled out is whether" — framing it as a question, not a declaration. This is the central causal mechanism of the episode's argument. Stating it as established fact creates a Law 5 violation and exposes the episode to simple debunking by any listener who checks the neuroscience.

Fix: Convert to question or acknowledged inference. "The place cells in the hippocampus encode spatial position using environmental signals — visual, spatial, vestibular. What the field has not fully answered is whether electromagnetic field orientation is among them, the way it clearly is in birds. The evidence is incomplete. The question is open. But the architecture is there."

### Repetition

### FINDING 8 — MINOR
**"prosecution" appears 33 times — structural necessity, but warrants awareness**

The word "prosecution" appears 33 times across the script, and "defense" appears with similar frequency. This is by design — the trial structure requires these terms. The repetition is structural, not lazy, and most instances are necessary to the form. However, in audio TTS delivery, the density of courtroom language in the verdict and closing sections can feel mechanical. This is a craft note for Guiding Light's consideration, not a required fix: in several consecutive paragraphs in the verdict section, "the prosecution" and "the defense" alternate so rapidly that the forensic-documentary voice risks sliding into courtroom procedural parody. A listener experiencing this on audio only, unable to scan ahead, may find the rhythm wearing.

This is a MINOR flag — fix only if Guiding Light agrees the closing section has the repetition problem on a listen-through.

### Sensory Grounding

### FINDING 9 — MINOR
**The Wardenclyffe Tower section has strong visual grounding but lacks temperature, smell, or sound**

Location: The passage describing Wardenclyffe construction ("a hundred and eighty-six feet of timber and steel rising from the potato farms of Suffolk County").

The atlas opening section is richly sensory — smell, texture, weight, temperature of the paper. The Wardenclyffe passage is primarily visual and structural. Suffolk County potato farms in 1901: the salt air off the Sound, the smell of turned earth, the sound of construction in a flat agricultural landscape — none of this is present. The tower is described architecturally, not experientially. For a major location that is the episode's central physical site, this is a gap.

Fix: Add one or two sensory details to ground Wardenclyffe as a place, not just a concept.

## DRAFTING ERRORS

### FINDING 10 — MINOR
**Encoding artifact in "Geográfico" — appears as "GeogrA fico" throughout the script body**

Location: All instances of "Instituto Geográfico Nacional" in the script body.

The character "á" (a-acute) has been corrupted throughout the script to "A " (capital A with space). In the script body the text reads: "Instituto GeogrA fico Nacional" and "The Instituto GeogrA fico Nacional." The Caption Key at the end shows the correct ARPAbet entry but the character corruption persists in every body instance.

This is a TTS-critical formatting issue. ElevenLabs will receive the corrupted text "GeogrA fico" and may mispronounce or misparse it. The correct text is "Instituto Geográfico Nacional" or, per Caption Key convention, the TTS-readable version "Instituto Geografico Nacional" (diacritics stripped).

Fix: Replace all instances of "GeogrA fico" in the script body with "Geografico" (diacritics stripped, per Caption Key convention for TTS delivery) or with the correctly encoded "Geográfico" if encoding can be confirmed clean.

## REVIEWER NOTES

**What this episode does exceptionally well:** The trial structure is clean and disciplined. The prosecution's case is genuinely persuasive — it is not strawmanned. The Inversion lands with precision: the prosecution proves the mechanism, the defense only needed the mechanism. The atlas opening and closing are the strongest sensory bookends in the series to date. The Morgan portrayal avoids cartoonish villainy and is more disturbing for it. The uniformity argument ("the misremembering predicts individual errors, not collective ones") is the episode's sharpest logical move and it earns the speculation that follows. The verdict section sustains the documentary tone through what could easily have become a rhetorical monologue.

**The two issues that need to be fixed before TTS:** The shaft depth (55 feet) and the journal name (Acta Psychologica) are both checkable facts that a motivated listener will verify within thirty seconds. Both are wrong.

**The issue that needs a decision:** The Ascension Island source is attributed to a real, operating, archivally-documented institution. The fabricated entries are described as having been "reviewed by researchers accessing the Observatory's historical archives" — a real archive that a curious listener can approach through the BGS or UK National Archives. This breaks the Attribution Rule. The fix is straightforward (genericize the location), but it is a structural choice that needs acknowledgment from the production team.

**The Mercator date** ("eighteen sixty-nine") will be spoken by ElevenLabs as 1869. Gerardus Mercator died in 1594. A listener who catches this will hear an impossible date for a historical figure and may dismiss the episode's credibility. This is the highest-priority fix for audio delivery.
