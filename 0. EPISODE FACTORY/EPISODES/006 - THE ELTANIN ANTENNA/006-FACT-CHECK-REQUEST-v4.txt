## SECTION B — The Photograph

**B1.** Photograph taken August 29, 1964.
- Verify: exact date

**B2.** Coordinates: 59 degrees 07 minutes South, 105 degrees 03 minutes West.
- Verify: exact coordinates
- **CRITICAL: This is in the SOUTH PACIFIC, west of Cape Horn. NOT the South Atlantic. Many fringe sources get this wrong.**

**B3.** Depth: 3,904 meters (12,808 feet).
- Verify: depth figure
- Note: The original New Zealand Herald article reported "2,250 fathoms" which converts to ~4,115 meters. The 3,904-meter figure comes from the scientific record (Heezen and Hollister). There may be a discrepancy between the newspaper and the scientific source.

**B4.** The ship was on its fifteenth Antarctic voyage at the time.
- Verify: voyage number

**B5.** The photograph was taken by an automated towed camera system — a camera housing lowered on a cable and dragged along the seafloor. Not operated by a person at the seafloor. Film developed hours later in the ship's darkroom.
- Verify: this was the standard deep-sea photography method in 1964

**B6.** First public mention: "Puzzle Picture From Sea Bed," New Zealand Herald, December 5, 1964. The ship docked at Auckland for overhaul.
- Verify: headline, newspaper, date, reason for Auckland stop
- LEGAL: specific publication citation

**B7. LEGAL — Attributed quote:** Dr. Thomas Hopkins described the object as "fairly symmetrical and the offshoots are all ninety degrees apart" and seemed "too extremely symmetrical to correspond to a natural formation."
- Verify: this quote or its substance is attributed to Hopkins in the NZ Herald article or other documented sources

**B8.** The object is approximately two feet (roughly 60 cm) tall. This estimate comes from the contemporary newspaper report.
- Verify: the size estimate and its source

## SECTION C — The Sponge Identification

**C1.** Identified as Cladorhiza concrescens (now Chondrocladia concrescens) by Bruce Heezen and Charles Hollister in their 1971 book The Face of the Deep.
- Verify: identifiers, book title, year
- LEGAL: real people, real book

**C2.** Heezen was a geologist at the Lamont-Doherty Geological Observatory (now part of Columbia University) who had worked on the Eltanin's research data.
- Verify: Heezen's institutional affiliation, connection to Eltanin data

**C3.** The species Cladorhiza concrescens was first described by zoologist Otto Schmidt in 1880 from specimens dredged from the Gulf of Mexico.
- Verify: original describer, year, location of original specimens

**C4.** Chondrocladia concrescens is a carnivorous deep-sea sponge in the family Cladorhizidae. It has a central stem with perpendicular branches. It captures small crustaceans with microscopic hooks.
- Verify: the species description, carnivorous nature, family classification

**C5.** The identification is accepted by marine biologists. No scientific paper disputes it.
- Verify: the identification's status in the scientific community

**C6.** Photographs of living Chondrocladia concrescens specimens show a softer, more irregular appearance than the 1964 photograph — more organic, ball-tipped branches rather than crisp geometric symmetry.
- Verify: visual difference between living specimens and the 1964 photograph is discussed in the literature

**C7.** No one has returned to the coordinates to verify the identification in situ. The object was not recovered.
- Verify: no documented return expedition
