**ISSUE F-07**
SEVERITY: CRITICAL
LOCATION: Script section on Tesla's essay — "Nineteen thirty. A long time after Wardenclyffe... Tesla published an essay in the New York American under the title 'Man's Greatest Achievement.'"
WHAT IS WRONG: The essay was published on July 6, 1930. This is correct — New York American, 1930. Tesla's essay is primarily about the nature of matter and energy using Vedic concepts, not about thought transmission or the brain specifically. The claim that Tesla wrote about thought having "a physical substrate that could, in principle, be measured, amplified, and transmitted" and called THIS "his most important theoretical contribution" does not appear to be supported by the available text of the 1930 essay.

The "great machine" quote ("the universe is simply a great machine which never came into being and never will end") IS a real Tesla statement, confirmed by Wikipedia. However it does not appear to be from "Man's Greatest Achievement" specifically — it may be from a different Tesla interview or essay. The exact source should be verified.

Additionally: the script refers to Tesla describing "the brain of man" as a "meat machine operating on resonance principles." Research confirms the "meat machine" phrase is primarily attributed to Marvin Minsky (via Joseph Weizenbaum, 1972 Science article), NOT to Tesla. Tesla used similar mechanistic body language but the specific phrase "meat machine" applied to the brain is a Minsky coinage, not a Tesla one. The brief states Tesla described the brain as "a meat machine operating on resonance principles." This is either unverified or misattributed.
CORRECT FACT: The 1930 essay "Man's Greatest Achievement" is about akasha/prana/Vedic cosmology. The "brain as meat machine" phrase is attributed to Marvin Minsky, not Tesla. The connection to "thought transmission" in the essay is not confirmed.
SOURCE: Open Tesla Research (full essay available), Quote Investigator (meat machine attribution to Minsky via Weizenbaum 1972), Wikipedia (Tesla "great machine" quote attributed without specific source).
RECOMMENDATION: MUST FIX. The script's description of the 1930 essay content is overstated. The "meat machine" framing for Tesla is dangerous — it conflates a Minsky phrase with Tesla. Either (a) remove the "meat machine" characterization entirely, (b) verify Tesla used this language separately from Minsky, or (c) rephrase to describe Tesla's mechanistic view of the human body without using that specific phrase. The "thought transmission" reading of the 1930 essay also needs qualification — the essay is about matter/energy in a Vedic frame, not explicitly about transmitting thoughts.

**CRAFT-01: Law 5 — Earned Declarations**
SEVERITY: IMPORTANT

Law 5 requires that speculative statements be earned by at least three real facts before the narrator makes a declarative leap. The following passages make declarations that may arrive before they are fully earned:

**Declaration 1:** "What happened to you is the same event that researchers are now studying in controlled conditions — the installation of a memory that arrived without a source." (Opening section)
STATUS: This arrives on the second page, before any research is cited. It is making a factual claim (this is what researchers are studying) before the researchers have been introduced. The reader is asked to accept the Loftus/CHI framework before it is established. This is a mild Law 5 violation — the declaration is "earned" later but arrives before the evidence.
RECOMMENDATION: This is a deliberate rhetorical technique (hook now, justify later) and is defensible as craft. Flag but do not require a fix — this is a judgment call. The opening section's power depends on this early declaration. If Guiding Light wants strict Law 5 compliance, move the declaration to after the Loftus section.

**Declaration 2:** "Something, for the past forty-five years at minimum, has been studying how to write directly to it." (Mid-script)
STATUS: This is stated as a near-fact. The evidence for "forty-five years at minimum" is the ARPA-IIP 1978 memo (fabricated) plus the 1961 radio study it references (also fabricated). The declaration is built entirely on a fabricated source. The real facts are: DARPA SMISC 2011, Finkelstein 2006-2009, Prosser 2005-2006. The real timeline is 15-20 years, not 45. The "45 years" claim derives from the fictional 1978 document's fictional 1961 reference. Presenting this as an established timeline is a Law 5 violation where a fabricated source is doing the work of three real facts.
RECOMMENDATION: SHOULD FIX. Rephrase to acknowledge the uncertainty: "If the reference number is real, the research program had been running for forty-five years." The script does use hedging language around the ARPA memo elsewhere ("may or may not exist") — apply the same hedging here.

**Declaration 3:** "The architecture is identical. The problem it solves is identical." (Near closing)
STATUS: This connects Tesla's Wardenclyffe system to AI social media as architecturally equivalent. The three real facts supporting this: (1) Wardenclyffe was designed for mass simultaneous transmission; (2) AI platforms distribute content simultaneously to millions; (3) both are described as operating on a transmission-to-receiver model. This is a reasonable architectural analogy stated as fact. It is an overstatement — the architecture is similar in function, not identical in design. "Functionally analogous" is more accurate than "identical."
RECOMMENDATION: SHOULD FIX. Change "The architecture is identical" to "The architecture is functionally identical" or "The problem is the same problem." "Identical" overstates the comparison.
