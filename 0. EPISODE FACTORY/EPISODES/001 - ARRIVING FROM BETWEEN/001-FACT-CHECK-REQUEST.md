## FACTS TO VERIFY

### 1. Birth
Nikola Tesla was born on July 10, 1856, in Smiljan, in the Military Frontier of the Austrian Empire (modern-day Croatia). His father was Milutin Tesla, a Serbian Orthodox priest. His mother was Đuka Tesla (née Mandić).

**Source cited:** Standard biographical record.

---

### 2. Death
Tesla died of coronary thrombosis on January 7, 1943, in Room 3327 of the Hotel New Yorker, Manhattan. He was eighty-six years old.

**Source cited:** Standard biographical record, New York Times obituary.

---

### 3. Discovery of body
Tesla's body was discovered on January 8, 1943, by maid Alice Monaghan, after a "Do Not Disturb" sign had been on the door for approximately three days (placed around January 5).

**Source cited:** Hotel records, biographical accounts. Please verify: (a) Was the maid's name Alice Monaghan? (b) Was the discovery date January 8, not January 7? (c) Was the sign placed approximately January 5?

---

### 4. Birth during lightning storm
Tesla was born at the stroke of midnight during a violent lightning storm. The midwife reportedly said the storm was a bad omen — a child of darkness. Tesla's mother reportedly replied the child would be a child of light. This is documented as family oral tradition, not meteorological record.

**Source cited:** Tesla's autobiography "My Inventions" (serialized in Electrical Experimenter magazine, 1919) and John J. O'Neill's biography "Prodigal Genius" (1944).

---

### 5. Geometric visions and compulsive counting
From early childhood, Tesla experienced involuntary visual phenomena: flashes of blinding light accompanied by visions. He also had an obsessive need for geometric measurement and division — counting steps, dividing objects into groups, needing arrangements divisible by three.

**Source cited:** "My Inventions" (1919) and W. Bernard Carlson's "Tesla: Inventor of the Electrical Age" (Princeton University Press, 2013). Please verify Carlson's book exists, is published by Princeton UP, and documents these compulsions.

---

### 6. Mental engineering quote #1
Tesla wrote: "It is absolutely immaterial to me whether I run my turbine in thought or test it in my shop."

**Source cited:** "My Inventions," Chapter II (1919). Please verify this is the exact wording from the original text.

---

### 7. Mental engineering quote #2
Tesla wrote: "Invariably my device works as I conceived that it should, and the experiment comes out exactly as I planned it. In twenty years there has not been a single exception."

**Source cited:** "My Inventions," Chapter II (1919). Please verify: (a) Is this the exact wording? (b) Does it say "twenty years" not "thirty years"? (c) IMPORTANT: This quote is NOT consecutive with quote #6 above — there are approximately four sentences between them in the original text. Please confirm they are separated.

---

### 8. Government seizure of papers
After Tesla's death, the Office of Alien Property (OAP) seized his papers and belongings — despite Tesla having been an American citizen since 1891. The seizure occurred within approximately two days of his death. The FBI's New York field office, under Assistant Director P.E. Foxworth, was involved.

**Source cited:** FBI declassified Tesla file (FOIA). Please verify: (a) Was it the OAP that seized the papers? (b) Was Tesla naturalized as a US citizen in 1891? (c) Was P.E. Foxworth the relevant FBI official?

---

### 9. John G. Trump review
Dr. John G. Trump, an MIT professor (and uncle of the future president), was assigned to review Tesla's papers. Trump's report concluded that Tesla's papers contained "no real significance to science or war."

**Source cited:** FBI Tesla file. Please verify: (a) Was Trump affiliated with MIT? (b) Is the "no real significance" quote accurate or paraphrased? What were Trump's exact words?

---

### 10. FBI file availability
The FBI's declassified Tesla file runs more than three hundred pages and is publicly available through the FBI's online FOIA reading room (vault.fbi.gov).

**Source cited:** FBI Vault. Please verify the file exists and is accessible at vault.fbi.gov.

---

### 11. The pigeon
Tesla had an intense attachment to a specific white pigeon with gray-tipped wings. When the pigeon died, Tesla reportedly said (per O'Neill's biography) that "something went out of my life." O'Neill also records Tesla describing a beam of light from the pigeon's eyes at the moment of death.

**Source cited:** John J. O'Neill, "Prodigal Genius" (1944). Additional documentation from Kenneth Swezey's correspondence (Smithsonian Institution Archives) and William L. Laurence (New York Times science writer).

Please verify: (a) Does O'Neill document the white pigeon with gray-tipped wings? (b) Is the "something went out of my life" quote in O'Neill, and is it exact or paraphrased? (c) Does O'Neill describe a beam of light from the pigeon's eyes? (d) Is Kenneth Swezey a verified Tesla correspondent with papers at the Smithsonian? (e) Is William L. Laurence a verified NYT science writer who covered Tesla?

---

### 12. Dane Tesla
Tesla's older brother Dane died as a child in Smiljan in a horse accident. Tesla described Dane as gifted — possibly more gifted than himself. The year of Dane's death is placed between 1863 and 1865 across biographies.

**Source cited:** "My Inventions" (1919). Please verify: (a) Was it a horse accident? (b) Does Tesla describe Dane as more gifted? (c) What year range do biographies give?

---

### 13. US citizenship
Tesla became a naturalized United States citizen in 1891.

**Source cited:** Standard biographical record. Please verify the exact year.
