## SECTION E — Radio Frequency Confusion

**E1.** Earhart transmitted voice on 3105 kHz. The Itasca could transmit voice on 3105 kHz but could NOT transmit voice on 6210 kHz.
- Verify: the frequency mismatch is documented
- Source claimed: Almon Gray, "Amelia Didn't Know Radio," Naval History Magazine, December 1993

**E2.** At 0843, Earhart said she was switching to 6210 kHz. She never returned to 3105.
- Verify: this is documented in the radio log

**E3.** The Almon Gray article exists: "Amelia Didn't Know Radio," Naval History Magazine, December 1993.
- Verify: article exists, author, publication, date
- LEGAL: specific publication citation

## SECTION I — The 157-337 Line

**I1.** "We are on the line 157 337" is a line of position — a navigational sun line. The numbers describe the line's compass orientation: 157 degrees (south-southeast) to 337 degrees (north-northwest).
- Verify: navigational meaning of the 157-337 line

**I2.** Extended southeast along the 157 bearing, the line passes through or near Nikumaroro (Gardner Island), approximately 400 miles from Howland.
- Verify: the 157-337 line's geographic extension toward Nikumaroro
- Source claimed: TIGHAR navigational analysis
