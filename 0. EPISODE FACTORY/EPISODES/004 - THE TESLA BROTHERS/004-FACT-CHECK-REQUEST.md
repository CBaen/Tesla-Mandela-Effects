# Episode 004 — The Tesla Brothers
## Fact-Check Request

**For:** Research verification before writing passes begin.
**Priority:** Items marked CRITICAL must be verified before writing. Items marked VERIFY can be confirmed during the Pre-Delivery Verification pass.

## CRITICAL — Verify Before Writing

### 1. Dane Tesla's Birth Year

**Claim in brief:** "born approximately eighteen fifty-one in Smiljan, Croatia" — five years older than Nikola (born July ten, eighteen fifty-six).

**Issue:** Secondary sources vary. Some biographies give eighteen fifty, some eighteen fifty-one, some eighteen fifty-three. The brief uses "approximately eighteen fifty-one" as a defensible hedge, but the five-year age gap with Nikola is asserted as fact.

**Verify:** What does the primary record show? Tesla's autobiography "My Inventions" (nineteen nineteen) — does Tesla state Dane's birth year or age at death? What do Marc Seifer's "Wizard" and W. Bernard Carlson's "Tesla: Inventor of the Electrical Age" give as Dane's birth year? If sources conflict, use the most defensible figure and hedge with "approximately."

**Stakes:** If Dane was, say, only two years older rather than five, the brother-pair dynamic changes and must be adjusted in the brief.

---

### 2. Competing Accounts of the Horse Accident

**Claim in brief:** Two competing accounts exist. Account one: young Nikola fired a pea from a blowgun that spooked the horse. Account two: the horse panicked unprovoked while Dane was grooming it.

**Issue:** The brief attributes Account one to Tesla's autobiography. Verify this. Does Tesla actually describe the pea-shooter incident in "My Inventions" as related to Dane's death, or does he describe the accident separately without self-implication? If Tesla implicates himself in the autobiography, that is significant. If the pea-shooter account comes only from secondary biographies, attribute it correctly.

**Verify:** Read the relevant passage from "My Inventions" (nineteen nineteen, Electrical Experimenter) directly. Specifically: does Tesla say the pea-shooter incident caused the horse to throw Dane, or are these two separate incidents described in the autobiography?

**Stakes:** The guilt dimension of this episode — the possibility that Nikola was responsible for Dane's death — is a major emotional layer. If Tesla does not implicate himself in the primary source, the episode should not assert it as Account one without noting the source's distance from the event.

---

### 3. Tesla's Description of Dane in "My Inventions"

**Claim in brief (direct quotes used):** — "I had a brother who was gifted to an extraordinary degree — one of those rare phenomena of mentality which biological investigation has failed to explain. His premature death left my parents disconsolate." — "The recollection of his attainments made every effort of mine seem dull in comparison. Anything I did that was creditable merely caused my parents to feel their loss more keenly."

**Issue:** These are attributed as exact, verified quotes. Verify they are exact — not paraphrased, not composites, not edited for length. The Quote Rule is non-negotiable.

**Verify:** Locate the exact passage in "My Inventions" (nineteen nineteen). Confirm word-for-word accuracy. Note the chapter and context so the writing agent can situate them correctly.

**Stakes:** Legal. Fabricated or inaccurate quotes attributed to real people are defamation. These must be exact.

---

### 4. Tesla's Description of Involuntary Visions

**Claim in brief:** Tesla describes "a peculiar affliction — the appearance of images, often accompanied by strong flashes of light, which marred the sight of real objects." The brief attributes this to "My Inventions."

**Issue:** Verify this is the exact quote and that Tesla connects this affliction to Dane's death, not to a separate cause. If Tesla attributes the affliction to something other than Dane's death in the primary source, the brief's framing needs adjustment.

**Verify:** Locate the passage in "My Inventions." Is the affliction explicitly connected to Dane's death, or does Tesla describe it without a stated cause? What does he say preceded its onset?

**Stakes:** The episode's central thesis — that Tesla's visualization method began as a grief symptom — depends on this connection being supportable from primary sources. If it is not explicit in Tesla's own words, it must be framed as interpretive (Layer 3 speculation), not as documented fact.

### 5. Orville Wright's Post-Wilbur Inventive Output

**Claim in brief:** "When Wilbur Wright died of typhoid in nineteen twelve, Orville effectively stopped inventing — he held patents and managed the company but produced no significant original work for the remaining thirty-six years of his life."

**Issue:** This is a strong factual claim used to support the brother-pair framework. Verify it.

**Verify:** What is the documented record of Orville Wright's inventive output after Wilbur's death in May nineteen twelve? Did he receive any significant patents post-nineteen twelve? What does the historical record show about his engineering activity after taking sole control of the Wright Company? The claim needs to be defensible — "produced no significant original work" is a strong statement that a listener could research.

**Stakes:** If Orville was moderately active post-Wilbur, the claim needs softening ("substantially reduced," "no patents of comparable significance," etc.). The analogy to Tesla is structurally important — if it breaks down under scrutiny, the brother-pair framework weakens.

---

### 6. Montgolfier Brothers' Parallel

**Claim in brief:** "The Montgolfier Brothers achieved the first human flight in seventeen eighty-three; neither achieved anything comparable alone."

**Verify:** Confirm the year (seventeen eighty-three) and that both brothers were involved in the first manned flight. What was each brother's role? Did either produce significant independent work after the partnership's primary period? Was there a split, or did they work together throughout their lives?

**Stakes:** The brief uses this as a parallel to the Wright Brothers. Confirm the parallel holds — that neither Montgolfier produced comparable work independently.
