## HANDLING NOTES — Critical for Script Execution

### The Collier's Weekly Tesla Quote

The exact words from the 1901 Collier's Weekly interview are documented and searchable. The brief provides the verified wording:

**Use this:** "While I was improving my machines for the production of intense electrical currents, the thought flashed upon my mind that I might be the first to hear the greeting of one planet to another."

**Do NOT do this:** Convert this to a quote about receiving signals "from Mars." Tesla's initial attribution to Mars is documented in other sources, but the Collier's Weekly quote is specifically about the possibility of being the first to hear interplanetary communication. The Mars attribution appears in later interviews and secondary accounts. Keep them separate. Use the exact Collier's Weekly quote in quotation marks. Narrate the Mars attribution without quotation marks.

**Flag:** Before using this quote in the script, verify the exact publication date of the Collier's Weekly issue. Multiple sources cite "1901" without a specific date. If the exact date (February 9, 1901 is most commonly cited) cannot be confirmed, present it as "in a nineteen-oh-one interview with Collier's Weekly" without a specific date.

### The Wardenclyffe Iron Root Depth

Tesla described his underground system at Wardenclyffe as connecting the tower to deep earth through a network of tunnels and iron pipes. The figure "three hundred feet" appears in some secondary sources. VERIFY this figure before using it. Tesla's own specifications for the earth-coupling system describe reaching the "Earth's natural reservoirs" — the specific depth figure varies by source. If "three hundred feet" cannot be verified, use "deep into the Long Island bedrock" without a specific number.

### Frequency Numbers — Use "Approximately"

Do NOT state any frequency as a precise figure without the qualifier "approximately." The scientific record uses:
- Schumann resonance fundamental: approximately 7.83 Hz (the fundamental; harmonics exist at higher frequencies)
- Tesla's calculated frequency: approximately 8 Hz (his notes give varying figures)
- The fabricated LHC baseline reading: 7.9 Hz (this is a fabricated figure — it is consistent with the real range but is NOT a verified fact; it belongs to Fabricated Source 1 only)

The proximity of these numbers is real. The exactness is not. Present the real numbers with "approximately." The fabricated number exists only within the fabricated memo and should not appear in the narrator's analytical voice as if it were independently verified.

### The July 4 Date Coincidence

This is REAL and VERIFIED. Wardenclyffe demolition: July 4, 1917. Higgs boson announcement: July 4, 2012. Ninety-five years.

Present this factually, without editorial language. "Remarkably," "strikingly," "coincidentally," "interestingly" — none of these. State the dates in proximity and let the listener respond.

Do not state that CERN chose July 4 to match the Wardenclyffe demolition date — there is no evidence for this, and stating it would be fabricating an intent that would undermine the episode's evidentiary integrity. CERN chose July 4 for its international recognizability (American Independence Day has international press traction). State the PR reasoning factually and let the date's resonance with Wardenclyffe do the work on its own.

### The Higgs Field Description

The Higgs field is what gives particles mass. "Decides what has substance" is the episode's interpretive description — it is earned as a closing paraphrase, not as a scientific definition. For the technical explanation in the Surface section, use accurate language: the Higgs field is the quantum field whose vibration produces the Higgs boson, and interaction with this field is what gives elementary particles their mass. "Decides what has substance" is a poetic compression of this, and it is appropriate in the closing/interpretive register — not in the analytical/investigative register.
