## What This Project Is

Tesla Mandela Effects is a faux-historical documentary podcast from a parallel timeline. NOT a conspiracy show. NOT a documentary essay series. It's audio fiction that presents itself as fact — movie-length investigations where the listener wears bodies, follows characters through moments that cost them something, and arrives at questions that don't resolve.

Each episode is 60-90 minutes of TTS audio (ElevenLabs Eleven Turbo v2), with AI-generated images (Ken Burns motion), published on YouTube. The audience is intelligent — history buffs, mystery enthusiasts, people who chose a 90-minute investigation over a 10-minute explainer. Treat them accordingly.

Three constants carry across 200+ planned episodes:
1. **The concept of Tesla** — present in every episode, directly or through his influence
2. **The Grid** — a geometric intelligence that entered through Tesla's birth, consuming this dimension. Made of squares (from Tesla's actual biography). Appears in 3 tiers: 60% implied, 30% threshold, 10% direct
3. **The narrator** — confused, intelligent, discovering alongside the listener. Not a tour guide. A researcher at 2 AM who can't make sense of what they found.

## What Happened in This Session

I arrived on March 17. Guiding Light had just listened to Episode 001 through ElevenLabs and found it boring. That word — boring — launched everything.

**Phase 1: Diagnosis.** I audited all 10 existing episodes. Average scores: Thesis 4.6/10, Dread 4.2/10, Tesla-as-non-human 2.4/10. Every episode was a well-written essay. None were stories. I sent 10 research teams to study narrative structure for long-form audio. The findings established the 7-point structure, the discovery narrator, the progressive commitment curve.

**Phase 2: Rebuilding.** I rewrote the Production Guide. I updated all 6 Episode Factory files. I wrote 5 episodes under the new architecture. GL reviewed Episode 001 and said it was still boring — better architecture, same essay voice.

**Phase 3: The real learning.** GL identified three forbidden narrator patterns I kept producing. I encoded these. I rewrote Episode 001. The editor Claude said: "Nobody bleeds." GL shared neuroscience research: when a narrator describes hands shaking, the listener's premotor cortex fires. When nobody in the episode has a physical experience, the listener receives information but doesn't simulate experience. Information doesn't hold ears for 90 minutes. Simulated experience does.

**Phase 4: Embodied writing.** I rewrote Episode 001 v4 with six embodied scenes where the listener is INSIDE a body. GL listened and said: "I'm really liking what I'm hearing." First positive signal in four days. I wrote Episodes 002 and 003 to the same standard. Both approved.

## The 21 Lessons (Read lessons-learned.md — ALL of them)

The most important ones:

1. **Documentary essays are not stories.** Information presented linearly is an essay. Stories have characters whose bodies things happen to.

2. **The narrator doesn't know the answer.** The narrator has the documents but not the meaning. Discovering with the listener — genuinely confused, genuinely curious.

3. **Three forbidden patterns.** (a) "We will come back to this." (b) Paragraphs about where a document is archived before saying what it contains. (c) Following a powerful line with paragraphs about why it's powerful.

4. **The first sentence creates a NEED, not a fact.** "The body hadn't even grown cold yet" — not "Nikola Tesla died alone in Room 3327."

5. **Embodied scenes, not explained information.** "The fork vanished. His hand was holding nothing." activates the motor cortex. "Tesla experienced flashes of light" informs the prefrontal cortex.

6. **Every fabricated character gets a real name.** Not initials. Not "Subject K."

7. **Fact-check the fact-checker.** Browser Claude nearly destroyed the best scene in Episode 001 with a wrong correction.

8. **The audience is intelligent.** Trust them.
