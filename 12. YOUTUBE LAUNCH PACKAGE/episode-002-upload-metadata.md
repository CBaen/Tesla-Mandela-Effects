## Description

In 1893, Nikola Tesla and Westinghouse lit the White City at the World's Columbian Exposition in Chicago. This historical docufiction episode follows Jackson Park, the Manufactures Building, staff construction, buried foundations, and the strange case of a temporary city that may not have behaved like one.

In 1893, the greatest buildings ever constructed in America were supposed to be temporary - plaster and wire over wooden frames, built to be demolished after six months. Nikola Tesla's polyphase AC system lit all of them. Then, inside the story, a demolition laborer named James Coburn puts his hands on the walls of the Manufactures Building and finds stone where plaster should have been.

Three archive fragments in the episode - a personal letter, a contractor incident report, and a 1904 soil boring survey - each point toward the same anomaly. None of them know about the others.

In the world of the episode, whatever Coburn's hands found is still below grade at Jackson Park. Below the Museum of Science and Industry. Unexcavated. Still unresolved.

Tesla Mandela Effects is a cinematic historical docufiction series investigating the hidden connections between Nikola Tesla's experiments and anomalies in the historical record. Each episode is standalone. Start anywhere.
